Short answer

The usual workaround is to switch the alarm off by hand before every holiday and remember to switch it back on. A better alarm works out your real workdays from your country and region: it skips public holidays, rings on official make-up workdays, and respects the time off you enter yourself.

It also has to be a real alarm. Look for one that schedules the upcoming rings with Apple's AlarmKit so iOS itself rings them, reads the schedule back before claiming it is ready, and treats a date it cannot confirm as a workday, so uncertain holiday data never silences a workday alarm. The holiday data should be built into EasyAM, so it keeps working offline.

What to look for before choosing

  • Public holidays skipped and make-up workdays rung, for your own country and region.
  • Real system alarms through AlarmKit rather than a notification that can be missed.
  • An unknown holiday date treated as a workday, never as a day off.
  • Weekly, alternating-week or shift patterns, plus your own time off.
  • Holiday data that works offline and shows how far ahead your alarms are set.

A practical decision process

  1. Set your working days or shift pattern, wake-up time, country and region once.
  2. Open the holiday calendar and check the next public holiday and make-up workday against your own calendar.
  3. Look at the date the alarms are covered through, and reopen the app to extend it.
  4. Add a day of your own time off and confirm the next alarm skips it.
  5. Keep a backup alarm for anything critical, as with any alarm app.

FAQ

Will it ring on a make-up workday?

Yes. Official make-up workdays are added to your schedule and ring even when they fall on a weekend; a date the app cannot confirm is treated as a workday.

Does it work offline?

Yes. The holiday data is built into the app, so alarms are scheduled without a connection; newer data can be downloaded when you are online.

Is it a real alarm?

It schedules the upcoming rings as individual alarms with Apple's AlarmKit, so iOS rings them, and it reads the schedule back from iOS before showing it as ready.
