An ATS CV in Germany: what the system reads before a person does

The classic German CV with a photo in the sidebar looks tidy — and applicant tracking systems often read it in the wrong order.

An ATS-friendly CV is one column, uses standard headings such as work experience, education and skills, keeps one date format, and is exported as a PDF with real, selectable text.

When you apply through a portal and upload a file, a parser reads it first, top to bottom and left to right. A sidebar with photo, contact details and skills gets read in between your job entries, and a tidy table turns into broken lines.

The tabular CV itself is not the problem — the column look is. Put period, position and employer on lines one under another rather than side by side. Use common headings: a section called "My journey" is often not recognised as work experience at all. And keep one date format, for example 03/2022 – 08/2025.

A photo is common in Germany but not required. If you include one, it should not force the layout into two columns. What to check before you choose

  • Whether the CV is one column, with no sidebar
  • Whether headings are standard terms such as work experience and education
  • Whether all dates use the same format
  • Whether PDF text is selectable rather than exported as an image
  • Whether contact details are in the body rather than a header or text box

The steps that actually work

  1. Check first whether you are uploading a file or filling in a form — layout only matters for uploads.
  2. Put every entry in one column: period, position, employer, one under another.
  3. Name sections with standard headings.
  4. Use terms from the job ad where they genuinely apply to you.
  5. Select a line in the exported PDF to confirm it is real text.

FAQ

Does a CV in Germany need a photo?

No, it is not required. Many people still include one. What matters to the system is that the photo does not force a two-column layout.

Is a tabular CV ATS-friendly?

Yes, as long as period, position and employer sit one under another in a single column, not in side-by-side columns or table cells.
