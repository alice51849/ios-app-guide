Pick a clock app that stays on automatically while charging, schedule a night face with dim red digits on true black, and lower the brightness until you can just read it from the pillow. On OLED phones, keep pixel shifting on. You never need to set Auto-Lock to Never, and unplugging won't drain the battery.

The usual first try is setting Auto-Lock to Never. Then you unplug in the morning, forget to change it back and the battery drains by lunch. StandBy only appears when the phone is charging on its side, so a different angle turns it off. A bedside clock needs to follow charging: on while the cable is in, back to the system setting when it's out, or on until the battery reaches a floor you choose.

The second issue is the light. A white face at 3 a.m. is basically a small lamp, and it wakes your partner too. It is easier on the eyes to switch automatically at bedtime to dim red digits on a true black background, with the brightness at the lowest level you can still read. OLED iPhones add one more worry: the same digits lit in the same place for eight hours can leave marks, so choose a clock with gentle pixel shifting and details that soften when idle.

The third is the morning. If the bedside clock also has an hourly chime and a simple alarm, you don't need another app; keep the chime for daytime if it would disturb you. Finally, placement. An iPhone standing upright fits a narrow nightstand; an iPad in landscape reads from across the room. 12 or 24 hour time, the date and weekday or a full month calendar can be set separately for each face, and you can switch to another time zone if family lives abroad. Wipe the screen before bed: fingerprints stand out on black.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether it stays on automatically while charging, without setting Auto-Lock to Never
  • Whether a night schedule switches to a dim red face on true black
  • Whether OLED screens get pixel shifting or similar protection
  • Whether the hourly chime and an alarm live in the same app
  • Whether it needs an account, shows ads or uploads data
  • Whether the face you pick also appears in StandBy, on the Lock Screen and in widgets

The steps that actually work

  1. Plug the iPhone or spare iPad you'll keep on the nightstand into the bedside charger.
  2. Turn on Keep Awake while charging in the clock app, or set a battery floor.
  3. Schedule the night hours so the face switches to dim red on true black before bed.
  4. Turn the lights off, lie down and lower the brightness until you can just read it.
  5. On OLED models leave pixel shifting on; turn the hourly chime on for daytime only.

FAQ

Is it bad for the battery to charge on the nightstand all night?

iPhone manages charging itself, and Optimized Battery Charging finishes topping up shortly before you usually wake. Use a cable in good condition and don't cover the phone. A clock that only stays on while plugged in returns to your system setting once you unplug.

Isn't StandBy enough?

StandBy only appears when the phone charges on its side. If it stands upright, sits on an iPad, or you want a fixed dim red night face and an hourly chime, a dedicated clock app helps. The Stay Clock face you choose also appears in StandBy, so both look the same.

How dim is dim enough for sleep?

Aim for the lowest level at which you can still read the digits from the pillow; red digits on true black are the gentlest in the dark. If you share the room, angle the screen toward your side of the bed.
