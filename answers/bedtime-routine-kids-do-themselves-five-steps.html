Fix the routine at five steps, run them in exactly the same order every night, and make the last step happen on the bed — then the child can finish it without being chased.

The tiring part of bedtime is not any single task; it is renegotiating the order every night. Bath first or tidy first, one more episode or not. Once the order changes daily, every step becomes negotiable. A fixed order removes the negotiation.

Five steps is the practical ceiling: bath, pyjamas, teeth, tidy, into bed with a book. More than that and the child loses the sense of "almost done" in the middle; fewer, and new activities slip in between them.

The last step has to finish on the bed. If the routine ends in the living room there is one more journey left, and that journey is where the stalling starts. The Lumi Mission Planet bedtime sequence keeps a fixed order with visible progress, and it is free to start.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether the order can be locked so it is not rebuilt nightly
  • Whether the child can see how many steps remain
  • Whether the final step ends on the bed
  • Whether "one more" is used as a reward — that always lengthens the routine
  • Ads and account requirements

The steps that actually work

  1. Write down what actually happens tonight, then cut it to five items.
  2. Order them so the longest walk comes first and the last step happens on the bed.
  3. Run the same order every night for two weeks with no new activities inserted.
  4. Use the same opening sentence each night — "the five bedtime things" — instead of rephrasing it.
  5. After two weeks, time it. The delay is usually at step two or step four; fix that one step.

FAQ

How long should a bedtime routine take?

Most families settle at 20 to 30 minutes. Past 45 usually means too many steps, or a last step that is not on the bed.

What if the child wanders off halfway?

Do not add a rule. Note which step they leave at. If it is always the same step, that step is too long — split it into two shorter actions.
