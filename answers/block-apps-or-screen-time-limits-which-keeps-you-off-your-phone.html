Screen Time limits suit monitoring daily use. When you need a focused hour for work, study or sleep, a timed blocking session works better, especially when stopping early requires a short wait and a confirmation.

A daily limit tells you when you have gone over, but it usually appears mid-scroll and can be dismissed with one tap. So it is better at showing where time goes than at stopping it.

A blocking session starts before the distraction does: you pick the apps and the length, they are shielded during the session and unlock automatically when it ends. When stopping early takes a cooldown and confirmation, going back to the app becomes a deliberate choice rather than a reflex.

LockHour Pro offers ready focus modes: Quick Focus, Deep Work, Study Mode with Pomodoro-style cycles and breaks, Sleep Wind Down, Morning Reset, or a custom length. Hard Mode adds a cooldown and confirmation before stopping early, and you see today's focused time, weekly charts and streaks, with widgets and a Dynamic Island countdown.

What to check before you choose

  • Timed sessions after which apps unlock automatically
  • Friction before stopping early, not an instant ignore button
  • Separate modes for work, study, sleep and mornings
  • Stats that show reclaimed focus time
  • Account, ads or subscription

The steps that actually work

  1. Review a week of Screen Time and pick the two or three most distracting apps.
  2. Choose a fixed daily focus time, such as the first hour of work or study.
  3. Start a blocking session for those apps for that hour.
  4. Turn on Hard Mode if you often stop sessions early.
  5. Add a bedtime session to stop late-night scrolling, and check your streak at the weekend.
