Blurry photos recover differently depending on the cause. Camera shake and soft focus respond well to sharpening, grain from dark rooms improves with denoise and low-light correction, but a subject that moved a long way during the exposure cannot be fully restored. Unblurry corrects by cause with eight modes, and you can press to compare before and after. Everything is processed on the iPhone, and photos are never uploaded.

Start by identifying the blur. If the whole frame is slightly smeared, it is camera shake; if the background is crisp and the face is soft, focus was missed; if a dark shot looks grainy, it is noise. Indoor party photos at the end of the year often combine darkness, noise and shake, and correcting them step by step looks more natural than a single pass.

Unblurry has eight modes: Auto Clear for everyday blur in one tap, Sharpen for soft focus and camera shake, Denoise for grain and high-ISO noise, Low Light to brighten dark shots, Document for text and whiteboards, AI Super Resolution where an on-device neural model rebuilds detail, 4× Upscale, and Portrait & Restore for faces and old photos. A before and after slider lets you spot overcorrection quickly.

Where the photo goes matters too. Few people want to upload a child's face or a family group shot to an unknown website. Unblurry is 100% on-device, so photos never leave the phone, with no subscription, ads or tracking. A holiday card photo or a snapshot of an old print can be finished on the iPhone in your hand.

What to check before you choose

  • Is there a mode for the cause: shake, missed focus, darkness or noise?
  • Can you compare before and after instantly?
  • Is the photo processed on the device rather than uploaded?
  • Do edges stay clean when enlarged (is there an upscale)?
  • Is there a mode suited to faces and old photos?

The steps that actually work

  1. Open the photo in Unblurry and start with Auto Clear to see the overall change.
  2. If shake or soft focus remains, use Sharpen; if it is dark and grainy, use Low Light and Denoise.
  3. For portraits, use Portrait & Restore for faces, and try the same mode on old photos.
  4. Press to compare before and after, and step back if it looks unnatural.
  5. For cards or prints, enlarge with 4× Upscale if needed, then save.
