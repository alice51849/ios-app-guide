Bopomofo for children in Brazil: where to start at home

Many families of Taiwanese origin in São Paulo and other cities want their children to read the way children in Taiwan do. The first step there is neither pinyin nor characters: it is bopomofo.

Bopomofo, also called zhuyin, is the system of 37 phonetic symbols used in Taiwan to teach Mandarin pronunciation before characters. There are 21 initials, 3 medials and 13 finals, plus marks for the four tones and the neutral tone. If your family has ties to Taiwan or your child will use books and materials from there, start with bopomofo; if the focus is mainland China, pinyin is the standard. At home, ten minutes a day, listening before writing, is enough to begin.

The big advantage of bopomofo for a Brazilian child is that the symbols do not look like Latin letters. With pinyin, a child tends to read "x", "q" or "zh" the Portuguese way and the pronunciation comes out wrong. With bopomofo, each symbol is a new sound, with no interference from the alphabet they are learning at school.

Tones are the hardest part for children growing up with Portuguese, because in Portuguese intonation changes the emotion of a sentence, not the meaning of a word. In Mandarin, mā, má, mǎ and mà are different words. That is why it pays to train the ear for tones from the start, with gestures or pictures that rise, fall or curve, rather than leaving them for later.

Once the child recognises single symbols, the next step is combining an initial, a medial and a final into a syllable, for example ㄍ + ㄨ + ㄚ. That is where reading really begins. Reviewing every day the symbols that still cause confusion works better than racing through all 37.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether the child hears the sound of each symbol, not just sees its shape
  • Whether writing follows the correct stroke order
  • Whether tones are trained by ear from the beginning
  • Whether there is a stage for combining symbols into syllables
  • Whether review brings back the symbols the child still gets wrong

The steps that actually work

  1. Pick a fixed ten-minute slot, for example after dinner
  2. Start with a few symbols with clearly different sounds and only listen and point
  3. Once the child recognises them by sound, move on to tracing each symbol in stroke order
  4. Practise the four tones on a single syllable, with up, down and curved gestures
  5. Combine initial, medial and final into simple syllables and review mistakes the next day

FAQ

Bopomofo or pinyin: which first?

It depends on where the child is headed. For ties to Taiwan or materials from there, bopomofo first. For mainland China, pinyin is the standard. Many children learn both later on.

At what age can we start?

Many families start around four or five with short listen-and-point games. Writing the symbols can come a little later.

Do parents need to speak Mandarin?

It helps but is not required for the first steps, as long as the child hears the correct pronunciation of each symbol and tone.
