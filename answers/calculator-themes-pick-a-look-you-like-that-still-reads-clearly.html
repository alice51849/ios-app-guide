Calculator themes: usable first, pretty second

When choosing a calculator theme, first check that digits and keys stay just as clear and easy to tap in that theme, then see whether the app remembers past results and lets you calculate straight from a Home Screen widget. The theme is what you see every day, but smooth calculating and results you can find again are what make you keep it.

A theme usually gives itself away in three places: low contrast between digits and dark or translucent keys, decorative key shapes that make you hit the wrong one, and thin result text that is hard to read outdoors or in bright light. Doing one real bill split or discount in a theme tells you more than any screenshot.

CalDaily has one hundred key themes built in, from quiet ceramics to deep prisms, each with its own key shape, material and light, and you can switch any time. The Home Screen widget calculates without opening the app, so you can tap out a sum right on the Home Screen.

It is also a calculator that remembers every result: name a result, and next week search for that shared dinner or the loan you compared instead of redoing it. History is grouped by date, can be filtered by tool, and exports to CSV for a spreadsheet. Eight tools, including tip and bill split, discount and tax, loan payments, unit conversion, date differences and working days, and trip fuel cost, live in the same app, and you set your usual tax rate and group size once.

What to check before you choose

  • In this theme, are digits and results still clear in bright light?
  • Besides looking good, are the keys easy to hit without mistakes?
  • Can you name past results and find them again by search?
  • Can the Home Screen widget calculate without opening the app?
  • Are there ads, and do you need an account?

The steps that actually work

  1. Pick three themes you like.
  2. Do one real bill split or discount in each and check the digits and keys.
  3. Keep the one that feels best and put the widget on your Home Screen.
  4. Set your usual tax rate and group size.
  5. Name important results as you go so you can search for them later.
