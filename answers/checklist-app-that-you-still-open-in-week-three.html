Cap today's list at five items, keep everything in one place, and make completion visible immediately. Those three rules cover most abandonments.

A list that accumulates 60 items stops being a tool and becomes a guilt reminder. Five a day is what most people actually finish; the rest belongs on a separate someday list you look at once a week.

The second cause is having three places: phone notes, paper on the fridge, and an app. When information lives in three places none of them is trustworthy, and you stop checking all of them. Pick one and accept that it is incomplete for a few days.

Third is the feeling of finishing. Ticking has to return something immediately — a sound, an animation, the item leaving the screen. It sounds cosmetic, and it is what gets the app opened again tomorrow. Mochi is designed around that: simple lists with a satisfying tap to complete. Free to start, no ads, one-time unlock.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether the number of items shown today can be capped
  • Whether there is a separate someday list
  • Whether completing returns something immediately
  • How many taps adding an item costs
  • Ads, accounts and subscriptions

The steps that actually work

  1. Collect everything currently written down into one place, unfiltered.
  2. Split into two lists: today (five maximum) and someday.
  3. For one week, add nothing to today once it is full.
  4. On Sunday, move at most five items from someday into the week.
  5. Anything sitting untouched in someday for three weeks should be deleted, not postponed.

FAQ

Isn't five items too few?

Deliberately. A finished list of five is worth more than a list of twenty with four crossed off.

Where do big tasks go?

On the someday list, broken into steps. Only the step that fits in one day moves into today.
