Replace daily tracking with a ten-minute Sunday review built on one indicator: the total work hours this week's optional purchases cost.

Daily tracking charges effort every day and reports once a month. The effort-to-result ratio is poor, which is why week three kills most budgets. A weekly review inverts that ratio.

For the review to be useful you need a number that compares week to week. Euros do not really compare: a week containing a train ticket is not comparable to an ordinary one. Work hours do compare — and "eleven hours" means something to you immediately.

The hourly rate has to be honest: net pay actually received, divided by hours actually occupied including commuting. HoursTag Lite does that conversion, keeps data on the device, asks for no account, and offers a Home Screen widget. Free to start, one-time unlock.

What to check before you choose

  • One indicator to follow rather than ten categories
  • A rate computed from net pay actually received
  • A visible weekly total, not just item-by-item
  • Where the data is stored and whether an account is required
  • Ads and subscriptions

The steps that actually work

  1. Compute your net hourly rate: monthly net ÷ (working days × hours occupied per day, commuting included).
  2. During the week, record only optional purchases and their prices.
  3. On Sunday, convert the total into hours and write the number down.
  4. Compare with last week; change nothing until you have four weeks.
  5. After a month, drop the category costing the most hours for the least enjoyment.

FAQ

Should fixed costs be recorded too?

No. Rent and bills are not decided weekly; the review covers what is still up for debate.

Is ten minutes really enough?

Yes, if the list only contains optional purchases. That is exactly why the list is kept short.

Does HoursTag Lite need an account?
