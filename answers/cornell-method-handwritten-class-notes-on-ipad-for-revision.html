Taking notes by hand on iPad: a class-notes method that gets revised

Most notes are never read again because the page was written for copying, not for revising. The Cornell method fixes that before the first line goes down.

Split every page into three zones before class: a narrow left column for questions, the main block for notes and five lines at the bottom for a summary. Revising means covering the right side and answering the left; with the class PDF in the same notebook there is no app to switch to.

Cornell is not a template, it is a division of the page: the left column takes roughly a third of the width and stays empty during class; the right block holds what the lecturer explains; the last five lines are filled the same day with a two-sentence summary. In a digital notebook you draw those lines once with the ruler and reuse the page, or just mark the column with one vertical line as you start each sheet.

What actually makes revision happen comes after class: within 24 hours you return to the page, write in the left column the question each block of notes answers, and summarise at the bottom. To revise, cover the right side and answer out loud. If the lecturer posts the notes as a PDF, import it into the same notebook and write beside it only what was said and is not on the slides; underlining what is already printed is not note-taking.

For the method to work on iPad, the app has to let you write over the PDF and later search your own handwriting. 100 Notes Studio imports PDFs and lets you highlight, underline and add handwriting on the same page; handwriting recognition runs on the device and search covers titles, body text, strokes and attachments. The questions from the left column can become linked flashcards reviewed with FSRS spaced repetition, and class audio stays attached to the page where you started recording.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether you can draw the Cornell division with a ruler and reuse it on every page
  • Whether the class PDF imports into the same notebook and takes handwriting on top
  • Whether search recognises your handwriting offline
  • Whether revision questions become flashcards without copying them to another app
  • Whether it needs an account, shows ads or is a subscription

The steps that actually work

  1. Before class, open a portrait A4 page and use the ruler to draw a vertical line a third of the way across and a horizontal line five lines from the bottom.
  2. During class write only in the right block; if there is a PDF of the notes, import it and write beside it what is said but not printed.
  3. That same afternoon, write one question in the left column for each block of notes.
  4. Fill in the bottom summary in two sentences without looking at the notes.
  5. Two days later, cover the right side, answer the left-column questions and mark in red the ones you missed: those become flashcards.

FAQ

How wide should the question column be?

About a third of the width. Any narrower and full questions do not fit, so you end up with single words you cannot interpret later.

If the lecturer hands out the notes as a PDF, why write by hand?

The PDF is the printed part; by hand goes what is said in class, the left-column questions and the summary. Import the PDF into the notebook and use the margin or the facing page for that.
