Discount calculation: one formula for each of the three common formats

Most discount mistakes are not arithmetic. They come from misreading which kind of deal the shop is actually offering.

Percent off is price times (100 minus the percentage) divided by 100; a second-item discount has to be averaged over both items; a spend threshold is counted in whole thresholds crossed and subtracted from the total, and all three take about ten seconds in your head.

The first step in any discount calculation is classifying the format. A straight percent off is the easy one: 20 percent off 1,280 is 1,280 minus 256, or 1,024. The mental shortcut is to divide by 10 first and then multiply: 128 times 8. In East Asian shops the same deal is written the other way round, as pay 80 percent, so a cart that mixes imported and local listings is where errors creep in.

The second format is second item 40 percent off, or buy one get one free. These must be read as an average unit price. Two items at 300, with the second at 40 percent off, total 300 plus 180, or 480, so each one costs 240: an overall 20 percent off, not 40. Buy one get one free averages 50 percent off, but only if you genuinely needed two. The third format is a spend threshold such as spend 1,000, save 100: count the whole thresholds crossed, and if you are 50 short of the next one, adding a small item is often cheaper overall.

Stacking order determines the final amount. The usual convention is item discount first, then the spend-threshold rebate, then coupons or cashback, but shops set their own rules, so check the small print before checkout. People who do this often want the results kept so this week's price can be compared with last week's. What to check before you choose

  • Whether the deal is percent off, a second-item discount or a spend threshold
  • For second-item deals, the average unit price rather than the second item's percentage
  • How far you are from the next spend threshold and whether adding an item is cheaper
  • The stacking order of item discount, threshold rebate and coupon
  • Whether the result can be kept and found the next time you compare prices

The steps that actually work

  1. Divide the price by 10, then multiply by the tenths you pay: 15 percent off means times 8.5.
  2. Second-item deals: add both prices, divide by two, and compare that with the full price.
  3. Spend thresholds: divide the total by the threshold, keep the whole number, multiply by the rebate.
  4. Stack in the order item discount, threshold rebate, coupon, then check it against the shop's rules.
  5. Write down the final amount with the item name so the next sale has a baseline to compare.

FAQ

Is pay 80 percent the same as 20 percent off?

Same amount, opposite phrasing. One describes what you pay, the other what you save; both come out to price times 0.8. Convert to one format before doing the arithmetic.

How much does second item 40 percent off really save?

An average of 20 percent over two items. It is only a saving when you needed both; buying a second item just to unlock the discount usually costs more.
