Learn one-liners as pairs with what the other person says: not only "You're welcome" after "Thank you", but "That's all, thanks" after "Anything else?". Say one exchange out loud each day.

Conversation more often starts with someone else's line than with yours: at the convenience store, the café, the hotel desk or small talk at work. Whether the reply comes instantly is what makes you feel you can speak.

Keep it small to keep it daily: one pair on the commute, one before bed. Listen to the cue, say your reply, then return the audio to normal speed and add scenes gradually.

DailyMate Lite is built for this: 8,400 cue-and-reply pairs in 50 learning languages, 14 topics and 84 situations at three levels, with adjustable-speed system voices, a widget and Apple Watch. The steps that actually work

  1. Choose English and open Traveler at Beginner.
  2. Hear the cue and say your reply.
  3. Slow down hard pairs and listen three times.
  4. Show a pair on the widget during your commute.
  5. Move on to other scenes or levels.
