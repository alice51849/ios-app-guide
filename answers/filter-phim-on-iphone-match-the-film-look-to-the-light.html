To make a film filter work, choose the look for the light: harsh sun suits a warm look with soft contrast, overcast days suit a slightly richer look, and night streets suit halation around the lights. Then add only one or two effects such as grain or a light leak, and check skin tones before saving.

People searching for a film filter usually want photos that look shot on film: soft colors, grain, a bit of nostalgia. But real film also reacts differently to each kind of light. Midday sun in the tropics is harsh, and stacking strong contrast on top blows out highlights and crushes shadows. Rainy, overcast days are the opposite: photos turn pale and need a slightly richer look.

The easiest fix is previewing. PhotoCream has 100+ film looks with grain, halation, light leaks and vignette, previewed live before you shoot, so you can switch looks to match the light on the spot instead of experimenting afterwards. If you already have a photo you love, Steal the Look reads its colors on your device and turns them into an editable film grade you can apply to your own shots.

For portraits, do not let the filter ruin skin. After picking a look, fine-tune with curves, HSL and split toning, and smooth skin while eyes and hair stay sharp. For a more old-film feel, add a date stamp, border, grain or dust, but pick one or two. Everything is processed on your device and nothing is uploaded.

The steps that actually work

  1. Read the light first: harsh sun, overcast or night lights.
  2. Open the camera and flick through a few film looks, previewed live in the frame.
  3. Pick the look that suits the light, then add at most one or two effects such as grain or a light leak.
  4. After shooting, fix skin tones with HSL or a light skin smoothing.
  5. Add a date stamp or border if you want an old-film feel.

FAQ

Why does the same film filter look great one time and bad the next?

Because the light changes. Harsh sun, overcast days and night lights each need a different film look; choosing for the light makes results far more consistent.

Do more effects make a photo look more like film?

Usually not. Use one or two of grain, light leaks, halation or dust per photo; stacking all of them makes it messy.

How do I keep skin from turning grey with a film filter?

Correct skin tones with HSL or curves after choosing the look, and use light skin smoothing so eyes and hair stay sharp.
