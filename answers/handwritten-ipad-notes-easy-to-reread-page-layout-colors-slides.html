Divide the page into three zones before writing: heading and date at the top, a narrow left column for questions, the right side for content. Use only three colours, each meaning one thing in every subject, and import the slide PDF into the same notebook as the notes. Revision then becomes a page-by-page pass that takes ten minutes.

Start with the page layout. Every page gets a heading printed in block capitals at the top, such as subject, week and topic, because block capitals are found by search far more reliably than cursive. Then draw a vertical line so the left column is about a third of the page; leave it empty during class, write content only on the right, and keep four or five lines at the bottom for a summary. When every page shares one layout, your eyes know where to look the moment you open it to revise.

Colours signal the type of information, not decoration. Three are enough: a main colour for content, red for definitions and likely exam material, a third for your own questions and thoughts. The meanings must stay the same in every subject; change them around and you stop trusting the colours within a month. Import the lecturer's slides as a PDF into the same notebook as your notes, then write beside them only what was said and is not on the slide. Highlighting what is already printed is not note-taking.

This only works when the app can find your handwriting. 100 Notes Studio recognises handwriting and searches on the device without a connection, across titles, text, handwriting and attachments. Slide PDFs import for highlights, underlines and handwriting on the same page. Questions from the left column can become flashcards and cloze cards reviewed on an FSRS spaced-repetition schedule, and audio recorded in class stays attached to the page where it started, so it plays back with that page's notes.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether it can search your own handwriting, and whether that works offline
  • Whether slide PDFs import into the same notebook as your notes and take writing on top
  • Whether a ruler or shape tool lets you draw the page division and reuse it
  • Whether nested folders and tags separate subjects and terms
  • Whether it needs an account, shows ads, and whether it is monthly or a one-time purchase

The steps that actually work

  1. Create one notebook or folder per subject and write the meaning of your three colours on its first page.
  2. On every new page, print the heading in block capitals at the top and draw the one-third left column with the ruler.
  3. Import the slide PDF before class; during class write only what the lecturer adds, beside the slide or on the right side of the page.
  4. The same day after class, write a question in the left column for each section of content and a two-sentence summary at the bottom.
  5. Every weekend, cover the right side and answer the left column; turn anything you cannot answer into a review card, then search a handwritten word to confirm the app finds it.

FAQ

How many colours should I use in my notes?

Three: a main colour, one for definitions and likely exam material, one for your own questions. Keep the meanings the same in every subject; more than that and you forget which colour means what.

If the lecturer already gives slides as a PDF, do I still need to write by hand?

Yes, but only what the lecturer adds and your own questions. Import the slides into the notebook and write beside them or on the next page; what is already printed does not need copying.
