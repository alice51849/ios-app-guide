Plan in blocks that match the limits: heavy work at the start of a fresh 5-hour window, lighter tasks and reading in between, and treat the weekly limit as a budget spread across the holidays rather than spent on day one.

Most holiday projects fail on rhythm, not ideas. Iterating for hours in one long chat spends a lot of your allowance on context sent again and again. Write a short spec first (goal, files, interfaces) and start each session with one clear task.

The 5-hour window is a natural work cadence. Put architecture, big refactors or debugging at the start of a window, and use the time until reset for testing, reading or time with family. The weekly limit is your budget for the whole break: split it roughly across the days and check which day it resets on your account.

That only works if you know where you stand. UsageNow shows Claude's 5-hour session limit and weekly limits, including per-model weekly caps, plus Codex, GitHub Copilot and Gemini, with a live countdown to every reset. Custom alerts at a percentage like 90 and a reset alert tell you when to pause and when to start again.

What to check before you choose

  • Which day your weekly limit resets on your account
  • How much of a 5-hour window a typical work block uses
  • Whether per-model weekly caps kick in earlier
  • A written spec before the first session
  • Tasks you can do without AI: tests, reading, cleanup

The steps that actually work

  1. Before the break, write goal, scope and file structure on one page.
  2. Split the weekly limit roughly across your free days and note the reset day.
  3. Start every 5-hour window with the hardest task, a clear prompt and lean context.
  4. Start new chats for new topics instead of dragging a long history along.
  5. At about 90 percent, pause, test and prep the next session until the limit resets.
