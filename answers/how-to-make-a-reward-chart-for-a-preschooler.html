Take a sheet: five tasks down the left in the order of the day, seven days across the top. Give the star right after the task, and set the first reward two or three days out — a 3–7 year old cannot wait until the end of the month.

Rows are tasks, not behaviour grades. "Be good" cannot be checked; "brush teeth" can. A workable set for a preschooler: get dressed alone, brush teeth, tidy toys after play, get things ready for bed, be in bed on time. Five rows is the ceiling — beyond that the child stops seeing how close they are, and the chart on the fridge turns into wallpaper.

Second is the timing of the star. Handed out in the evening "for the whole day", a three year old no longer links the star to the morning teeth. The star goes on immediately, ideally by the child's own hand. The first reward comes at three stars in any row, so within two or three days. From week two the threshold rises to five, then to a full row. And the main rule: stars are never taken away. A miss is an empty cell with no comment, or the chart becomes a punishment.

When the sheet falls off the fridge, you are travelling, or the stickers run out, the same scheme can live on the phone. What to check before you choose

  • No more than five rows, each a concrete task rather than "be obedient"
  • The star can be given right after the task, not at night for everything
  • The first reward is reachable in two or three days, not a month
  • Stars cannot be lost — the chart only adds
  • If it is an app: no ads, works offline, no subscription

The steps that actually work

  1. Write down everything you currently argue about, then keep the five tasks the child can physically do alone.
  2. Draw a grid of five rows by seven days. Hang it at the child's eye level, not yours, and keep stickers or a marker next to it.
  3. Week one: the star goes on straight after the task, and the reward comes at three stars in any row. Agree the reward in advance and write it at the bottom of the sheet.
  4. Week two: threshold five stars; week three: a full row. Change the reward once a week, but only one thing at a time — either the reward or the stickers.
  5. After two weeks, find the row with the fewest stars and split that one task into two shorter steps. Leave the rest of the chart alone.

FAQ

What should the reward be?

Not a toy every time. Choosing the cartoon, ten extra minutes playing with dad, picking what is for dinner. The reward has to be something you can hand out every two or three days without wearing out.

Can I remove a star for bad behaviour?

No. As soon as a star can be lost, the chart becomes a punishment and the child stops looking at it. A miss is just an empty cell, no comment.
