Capacity drops as cycles accumulate; Apple's design target is 80 percent of original capacity at 1,000 cycles for iPhone 15 and later and 500 for earlier models, so today's percentage plus your cycle count gives a rough time to 80.

What people call battery capacity is the Maximum Capacity figure under Settings > Battery > Battery Health. A new battery is 100 percent, and the number shows how much of that the battery still holds. Once it drops below 80 percent iOS suggests battery service, and well before that the day-to-day runtime gets noticeably shorter. So the useful question is not what it is now but how many points it loses per month.

The first variable is cycle count. Using and recharging the equivalent of 100 percent is one cycle, so someone who drains to empty daily accumulates cycles faster than someone who stays between roughly 30 and 80. The second variable is heat. Charging in a hot car or fast charging while gaming wears capacity independently of cycles. That is why cycle count and thermal state need to be recorded together before the cause becomes visible.

The simplest way to get a trend is to write down Maximum Capacity and cycle count once a month. If you hand that to an app, it should be clear what it reads and from where. BattAI uses five values from public iOS APIs, battery level, charge state, Low Power Mode, whole-device thermal state and time, calculates the rest from those readings, and shows the source and limits of each detail. It labels every other result as estimated or provided by you, and it can create a battery report for service, resale or your own records as a PNG or a plain-text table.

What to check before you choose

  • Today's Maximum Capacity and how it compares with last month
  • Cycle count, and how many cycles a day you are adding
  • When the phone gets hot during charging
  • Whether the app shows where each value comes from
  • Whether readings leave the device, and whether there are ads or tracking

The steps that actually work

  1. Open Settings > Battery > Battery Health and note Maximum Capacity with the date.
  2. Note the cycle count the same day; repeat a month later and compare both.
  3. Pick a charge range: top up often between about 20 and 80 percent instead of draining to zero.
  4. Stop charging when the phone is hot; a sunny dashboard mount or fast charging mid-game are the usual cases.
  5. After three months, divide the points still above 80 by the monthly loss to estimate how long you have.

FAQ

Do I have to replace the battery at 80 percent?

Not immediately. Eighty percent is where iOS begins recommending service; whether the phone lasts your day is the real test. If the decline is fast, plan the replacement earlier.

Where do I find the cycle count?

iPhone 15 and later show it under Settings > General > About. Earlier models expose it only in analytics data files, so check what an app keeps when it reads that file.

Does BattAI send any data anywhere?

No. There are no ads, analytics, tracking, third-party SDKs or internet requests; data stays on the device apart from a minimal snapshot sent directly to a paired Apple Watch, and battery reports are created on the device without upload.
