Battery wear comes down to two numbers: maximum capacity, the charge the battery holds now compared with when it was new, and cycle count. Maximum capacity appears under Battery in the iPhone's Settings, and dropping below 80% is the usual point to consider a replacement. A trend over several weeks says more than one reading. BattAI shows a battery health score, what shapes the score, and maximum capacity over time, with the source and limits of each detail. Data stays on the device, with no network requests or ads.

Maximum capacity treats the new battery as 100% and shows how much it can hold now. The lower it goes, the sooner the same day of use runs it down, and the more likely the phone is to shut off on cold days or under heavy apps. When buying used or trading in, read this number together with cycle count. Large wear on few cycles can point to heat or charging habits.

Then the trend. Maximum capacity wobbles from day to day, so following several weeks is easier to judge than one look. BattAI shows a battery health score plus what shapes it, maximum capacity over time, charge care, charge range and charge rate. It uses five values from public iOS APIs: battery level, charge state, Low Power Mode, thermal state and time; other results are calculated from those readings or added by you, and each detail shows its source and limits.

When selling or handing a phone on, showing a record moves faster than saying "the battery is fine". What to check before you choose

  • Can you record the trend, not just today's maximum capacity?
  • Can you see maximum capacity as a trend over time?
  • Are the basis, sources and limits of the score explained?
  • Is data kept on the device rather than uploaded?
  • Can you turn the record into a battery report (image or table) to show someone?

The steps that actually work

  1. Check today's maximum capacity under Settings > Battery and note it.
  2. Open BattAI and look at the battery health score and what shapes it.
  3. Record maximum capacity and cycles, and review the trend every few weeks.
  4. Use charge care and charge range to review everyday charging.
  5. Before a trade-in or sale, create a battery report and keep it as a PNG or a plain-text table.
