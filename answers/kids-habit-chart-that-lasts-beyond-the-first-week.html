Cut the list to three items, anchor each one to a moment that already happens every day, and make the result visible the second the child finishes — that is what gets a chart past week one.

Preschool and early-primary children remember sequences, not lists. So the first step is not writing down the ten things you wish they did; it is finding three moments that already happen every day (after waking, after a meal, after the bath) and hanging one new habit behind each of them.

The second failure point is delayed feedback. A sticker sheet that pays out at the end of the week is too far away for a five-year-old. Something visible has to change the moment the task is done; weekly totals come after that.

Lumi Mission Planet is built in that order: short daily missions, an immediate response when one is finished, and a fixed bedtime sequence in the evening. It is free to start, the full content is a one-time unlock, and there are no subscriptions and no ads.

What to check before you choose

  • How many items per day — three or fewer, or an adult ends up nagging
  • Whether each item is anchored to an existing moment rather than "sometime today"
  • Whether finishing changes something visible now, not only at the weekend
  • Whether the child can operate it without an adult tapping for them
  • Ads and accounts — the two things to check first in any app for children

The steps that actually work

  1. For three days, write down what the child already does at fixed times, and pick three of those moments.
  2. Attach exactly one new habit to each moment, phrased in words the child uses: "put the cup back after brushing".
  3. Let the child do the finishing action; the adult only watches.
  4. Add nothing new for seven days. On day eight, look at which item keeps getting skipped.
  5. A item that keeps getting skipped usually has the wrong anchor. Move it instead of adding a reward.

FAQ

What age is a habit chart useful?

Roughly three and a half to eight. Younger children cannot follow the sequence yet; older ones can usually use a plain written list.

Do we have to give a physical reward?

No. Immediate feedback matters more than a prize, and swapping the prize for "we do something together" usually lasts longer.
