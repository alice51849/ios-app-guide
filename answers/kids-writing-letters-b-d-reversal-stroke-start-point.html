Your child writes letters backwards: use the starting point to tell b, d, p, q apart

When a child writes b as d or p as q, many parents worry something is wrong. In fact it is very common before age seven: these letters share one shape and differ only in direction, so remembering them by how they look naturally leads to reversals.

When your child writes letters backwards, do not just say “look carefully”; fix each letter's starting point and first stroke instead: b starts with the line and then adds the bump on the right, d starts with the circle and then adds the line on the right. Practise only one confusable pair a day: trace three times in stroke order, write twice in a guide grid, and if it reverses, start again from the starting point.

Lowercase b, d, p and q are one shape turned in different directions. A child who remembers “a stick and a circle” struggles to also remember which side the circle goes on. If the starting point changes every time, the hand has no fixed movement to rely on, and reversals become more likely. Shifting attention from what it looks like to where it starts and where the first stroke goes fixes the hand movement, and direction follows.

Practise one pair at a time, such as b and d first, then p and q once those are steady, not all four together. The steps that actually work

  1. Notice which pair your child reverses most, for example b and d.
  2. Fix the start in one phrase: b is “line first, then circle”, d is “circle first, then line”.
  3. Trace three times in stroke order, always from the same starting point.
  4. Write twice alone in a guide grid; if it reverses, point to the starting point and rewrite once.
  5. When the pair is right several days in a row, move to the next pair, such as p and q.

FAQ

Is writing b as d a learning disability?

Reversals are very common before age seven, usually because the shapes are similar and direction is not fixed yet. If they are still frequent in the middle primary years, or come with reading difficulties, ask a teacher or specialist to assess.

Should I keep correcting reversals?

No long corrections are needed. Point to the starting point, say “start here”, and let your child write it once more; the goal is a well-practised first stroke.

Does tracing letters on an iPad help with writing on paper?

Tracing fixes stroke order and the starting point, but holding a pencil on paper still needs its own practice. Trace on screen, then write once on paper; doing both works best.
