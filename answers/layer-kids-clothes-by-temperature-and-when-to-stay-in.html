Layer by feels-like temperature: one layer above 25 degrees, two from 15 to 25, three from 5 to 15, three plus hat, gloves and scarf below 5. Ten minutes after going out, touch the back of the neck: warm and dry is right. Feels-like above 35, AQI above 150, thunderstorms or strong wind mean a day at home.

The point of layering is that each layer has one job. The inner layer wicks sweat, so cotton or modal rather than synthetics. The middle layer warms: fleece, a jumper or a thin down liner. The outer layer blocks wind and rain: a shell, windbreaker or raincoat. Two thin layers beat one thick one because the air between them insulates, and one can come off in a mall or on the metro without going straight from padded coat to T-shirt.

The chart assumes a toddler who runs around. Above 28 degrees, short sleeves and shorts with a sun hat. 23 to 28, one long- or short-sleeved layer with a light jacket in the buggy. 18 to 23, long sleeve plus light jacket or gilet. 12 to 18, base layer, jumper and windproof jacket, a thin hat if windy. 5 to 12, base layer, jumper, heavy coat, hat and gloves. 0 to 5, thermal underwear, fleece, down or padded coat, scarf. Below 0, add thick socks and a buggy footmuff, and keep each outing under 30 to 45 minutes. A baby who is carried or lying in the buggy gets one layer more than the chart; a bigger child who never stops moving can take one off.

Most mistakes are not the chart, they are reading only the air temperature: wind, humidity and a missing sun pull the feels-like down several degrees. What to check before you choose

  • Feels-like temperature for the hour you go out, not the reading at wake-up
  • Wind: at force 5 or more, move the windproof layer outside or add one
  • Rain chance for the next hour, because getting wet steals heat faster than cold does
  • Whether the child walks, rides in a buggy or sits in a carrier
  • Air quality index: above 100 shorten the outing, above 150 stay in

The steps that actually work

  1. The night before, open the hourly forecast and note the feels-like temperature and rain chance for the hour you plan to go out, ignoring the daily figure.
  2. Lay out three layers from the chart: inner for sweat, middle for warmth, outer for wind and rain.
  3. Add a layer for buggy or carrier; take one off for a child who runs and climbs.
  4. Ten minutes out, touch the back of the neck: warm and dry is right, damp means a layer off, cool means hat or jacket on. Cool hands and feet do not mean cold.
  5. Put the removed layer in the buggy or backpack, not around the child's waist; it goes back on when the evening cools on the way home.

FAQ

Does the old 26-degree clothing rule still work?

Only as a rough guide. It assigns a fixed warmth value to each garment and ignores wind, humidity and how much the child moves. Layering by feels-like temperature and checking the neck is more reliable than adding up garment numbers.

Which days should we simply stay home?

Feels-like above 35 or below minus 10, AQI above 150, thunderstorm or heavy-rain warnings, and wind at force 6 or more. Those days are not solved by dressing right; switch to indoor play.
