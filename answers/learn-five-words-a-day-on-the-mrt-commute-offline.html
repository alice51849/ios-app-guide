Cut the daily dose to five words, attach it to a slot that already exists (the subway ride to work), and make sure it needs no internet and no preparation; that is how a streak survives. Wordmate Lite: Vocabulary is built around exactly that: open it, walk five words, done, offline, no account.

Stopping on day three is rarely laziness; it is usually the wrong dose. Day one is enthusiastic and you do thirty words. Day two you are rushed, manage ten, and feel you have lost. Day three you do not open the app. Drop the daily goal to five words so that even the worst day is finishable, and the streak stays alive, which matters far more than one heroic day of twenty.

The second key is tying practice to a moment that needs no decision. Between tapping in and tapping out on the subway, queueing at the convenience store, waiting for the bus: these one-minute gaps already exist, so you never have to find time. Wordmate Lite: Vocabulary is designed for those gaps. The screen is a trail of pebbles; step over five and the day is done, with the closing screen listing exactly the five words you just learned. Everything runs offline, so a tunnel with no signal changes nothing, and there is no account and no personal data collected.

Third, do not build your own flashcards. People restarting a language most often stall at sorting out a deck first, and never start. Pick a path you will actually use, business for a trip for work, travel for next month in Tokyo, begin at beginner level, and save any word worth keeping with a tap; saved words are grouped by topic and level. The free tier keeps one language and one path open at beginner level, enough to build the habit; when it sticks, a single one-time purchase unlocks all 44 languages, 14 paths and every level, with no subscription.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether the daily goal can be set as low as five words
  • Whether it still works with no signal on the subway or a plane
  • Whether it tracks a streak and shows exactly what you learned today
  • Whether you must build flashcard decks before you can start
  • Who reads the words aloud, and whether you can choose the voice

The steps that actually work

  1. Pick a moment that already happens every day as the trigger, such as tapping through the subway gate in the morning.
  2. Set the daily goal to 5 words and leave it there for the first two weeks.
  3. Choose one language and one life path and start at beginner level; do not open too many at once.
  4. After the trail, glance at the five words listed and save the ones worth keeping; they are filed by topic and level for later.
  5. Only after seven days in a row, consider raising the goal to 10.

FAQ

Are five words a day really enough?

Enough is whatever you can sustain. Five words for thirty days is 150 words, far more than thirty on day one and then nothing. Once it feels easy, the daily goal can go to 10, 15 or 20.

Can I learn Japanese and Korean in Wordmate Lite: Vocabulary?

Yes, there are 44 learning languages. The free tier keeps one language and one path open at beginner level; the rest stay visible so you can see what is there, and one purchase unlocks all of them.

Does it freeze when the subway has no signal?

No. All content works offline, and pronunciation uses the device's own text-to-speech voice, so words are still read aloud without a connection.
