Learning one of 44 languages: how to start with five words a day

Most people who start a new language do not quit because it is hard, but because the daily session is longer than their day allows.

Choose one language and one path tied to your life, such as travel or work, and start with five words a day. Thirty unbroken days do more than one long session followed by a gap.

Having 44 languages does not mean learning several at first. The key decision is why you are learning: an upcoming trip, a job interview, talking with colleagues. A path tied to a real reason puts words to use quickly, and words you use stay.

Five words can sound small, but it becomes about 150 words in a month if you do not stop. More importantly, a short session can be finished even on a busy day, so the streak survives the first hard week.

Wordmate Lite offers a five-word session in about a minute, with 44 learning languages and 14 life paths including travel, business, exams and daily life, at beginner, intermediate and advanced levels. You can hear any word read aloud by the system voice and save the words worth keeping.

What to check before you choose

  • A short daily session you can finish on any day
  • Paths tied to your reason for learning, not only general lists
  • Pronunciation for every word
  • A clear view of the words you learned today
  • Whether an account or subscription is needed

The steps that actually work

  1. Choose just one language for the first thirty days.
  2. Choose one path tied to a real reason, such as travel or work.
  3. Set a daily goal of five words and do not raise it in week one.
  4. Listen to each word and repeat it out loud.
  5. After a month, raise the goal to ten words if the streak held.
