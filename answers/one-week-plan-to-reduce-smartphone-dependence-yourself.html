Days 1–2: record your top three apps and daily pickups in the Screen Time report. Days 3–4: lock just one 25–50 minute block a day. Day 5: add the hour before bed. Day 6: add the first 30 minutes after waking. Day 7: compare with day 1 and keep the rules that worked.

The first two days are only for measuring. Open Settings, then Screen Time, and note the three apps you use most and how many times a day you lift the phone. Do not change a habit yet. Most people discover the time is going to one or two apps rather than five, and once you know the opponent, the task gets narrow.

From day 3, lock one time slot. Choose a 25–50 minute stretch you already wanted for focus, such as after lunch or eight in the evening, lock the top three apps only for that slot, and let them unlock automatically. The point is this slot, not forever: when the brain knows the apps return in an hour, it resists far less. Days 5 and 6 add the hour before bed and the thirty minutes after waking, the two bands of the day where phone time stretches most easily.

On day 7, compare with the first day. Did pickups drop? Did the top three apps lose minutes? Keep the rules that worked, delete the ones that did not, and add one more next week. What to check before you choose

  • Whether you know your own daily pickups and the minutes on your top three apps
  • Whether the lock has an end time and releases on its own when it arrives
  • Whether stopping early meets a cooldown or a typed confirmation
  • Whether there are bedtime and morning modes so you are not setting things up by hand each time
  • Whether a weekly chart lets you confirm the change in numbers on day 7

The steps that actually work

  1. Days 1–2: in Settings, open Screen Time and note the top three apps and pickups. Leave habits alone.
  2. Days 3–4: lock the top three apps for one 25–50 minute slot a day and let the automatic unlock handle the end.
  3. Day 5: add a lock for the hour before bed and charge the phone away from the bed.
  4. Day 6: keep social apps closed for 30 minutes after waking; finish one planned task before unlocking.
  5. Day 7: compare with day 1, keep only the slots that worked, and add just one more next week.

FAQ

Should I delete my social apps?

You do not have to, and most people who delete them restore them within three days. Locking a time slot is easier to keep up for a week, and because the apps come back when the slot ends there is no sense of deprivation.

I locked on day 3 and still want to unlock.

Shrink the slot to 25 minutes first, then extend it bit by bit once you get through. If a single tap to unlock is too easy, turn on Hard Mode: a cooldown and a typed confirmation come before stopping early, so unlocking becomes a deliberate choice.
