Depending on how it was encrypted, a password-protected ZIP may not extract with the iPhone's built-in tools, and a RAR or 7z will not open at all. Open the archive from the share sheet in an extractor that works on the device, paste the password from the second email, then look inside and take out only the files you need. Zipbox: Unzip RAR, ZIP & 7z never sends the file anywhere and makes no network requests, so a client's documents never go to an online unzip site.

Many Japanese companies still send attachments as password-protected ZIPs and mail the password in a second message, a routine known locally as PPAP. It is awkward away from a desk: tapping the attachment in Mail shows nothing useful, and saving it to Files does not open it either. Start by long-pressing the attachment, choosing Save to Files, and checking that the archive really landed on the device.

Then pick an extractor that accepts a password. Two things matter: explicit support for password-protected and multi-part archives, and processing that stays on the phone. Zipbox handles password-protected and multi-part ZIP, RAR and 7z, lets you browse the file list and preview photos, PDFs and text before extracting, and pulls out just the item you choose. It is a paid App Store download where one purchase covers every feature, with no in-app purchases, subscription or ads.

Finally, handle the content with care. Files arrive password-protected because they are quotes, contracts or lists with personal data. Uploading them to an online extractor hands both the password and the contents to a third party. Extracting on the phone keeps the file and the password on the device, and the extracted PDF can be forwarded from the share sheet.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether the attachment was actually saved to Files with Save to Files
  • Whether the extension is .zip, .rar or .7z, and whether it is split into .001 or .part1 volumes
  • Whether the app states support for password-protected and multi-part archives
  • Whether extraction stays on the device instead of uploading to a server
  • Whether an ad or countdown stands between you and the file

The steps that actually work

  1. In Mail, long-press the ZIP attachment and choose Save to Files, into iCloud Drive or On My iPhone.
  2. In Files, long-press the archive, tap Share and choose Zipbox, or open that folder from inside Zipbox.
  3. Paste the password from the second email; copying avoids typing mistakes.
  4. Browse the list, preview photos or PDFs, and tick only the files you actually need before extracting.
  5. Send the extracted files from the share sheet to Mail or Files and forward them to your team.

FAQ

The password is right but the archive still will not open.

Copy the password from the email instead of typing it; full-width characters or stray spaces are the usual cause. For split archives, keep every part in the same folder.

Does Zipbox send the archive to a server?

No. Zipbox makes no network requests and needs no account or sign-in; extraction and preview happen on the device.

How do I forward the extracted PDF?

Open the extracted file from the share sheet and send it to Mail, Files or iCloud Drive.
