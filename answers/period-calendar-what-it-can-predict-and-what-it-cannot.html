A period calendar estimates your next period, fertile days and ovulation day from your previous cycles. It is a calculation, not a diagnosis: the error is larger with irregular cycles, and it is not contraception.

The estimate rests on one thing: the length of your past cycles, counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. After one or two logged cycles there is too little data, so the calendar leans on general averages. After three or four, the estimate reflects your own rhythm.

Fertile days and ovulation are counted back from the expected next period, so any error in that date carries over to them. If your cycles vary by a week, the calendar will show a wider window than for someone with even cycles. Stress, flights and illness shift a cycle, and no calendar can foresee that.

So expect preparation from a calendar, not guarantees: knowing when to carry supplies, when cramps or mood may change. Cyca estimates your next period, fertile window and ovulation day and updates the estimate every time you log; cramps, flow and mood take seconds to record. What to check before you choose

  • Whether estimates come from your own records, not a fixed 28 days
  • Whether the estimate updates after every log
  • Whether cramps, flow and mood can be logged alongside the dates
  • Whether there is a reminder before the next period
  • Whether an account is required and where data is stored

The steps that actually work

  1. Log the first day of your period on the day it starts.
  2. Treat the estimate as a guide, not a date, for the first three or four cycles.
  3. Log cramps and flow next to the dates — that is how your own patterns show up.
  4. Turn on the reminder before the expected period so you can prepare.
  5. If your cycle changes sharply or periods stop for several months, see a clinician.

FAQ

How many cycles before the estimate gets closer?

At least three or four. Before that, the calendar relies more on general averages than on your rhythm.

Can a period calendar be used to avoid pregnancy?

No. A fertile-day estimate is a calculation with error, not a method of contraception.

Does Cyca need an account?

No. No account, no cloud, no tracking: data stays on the device and the app works offline.
