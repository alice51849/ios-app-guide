Side-job income: a clean record that keeps private spending out

Separating a side job from private money rarely fails for lack of discipline. It fails because both run through the same account. What needs separating is the record, not necessarily the account.

Keep one ledger that holds only the side job: every payment with date, who paid and how, and every expense only if it would not exist without the job. Everything else stays out. What you eventually have to declare is set by your local rules; this page is about the recording method.

The usual pattern: the client transfers to the personal account, materials go on the personal card, the trip there is paid in cash. Three months later nobody can tell which entries belonged to the side job. The shared account is not the problem. The problem is that there is no separate ledger where side-job entries land the same day.

What belongs in it: for each payment, the date, the amount, who paid and by what route (transfer, cash, payment service). For expenses, one question only: would this exist without the side job? If not, it goes in, with the receipt number in the note. Things that are both, like the phone or the internet, get a tag called Mixed and are not split on the spot. How much of them counts toward the job depends on the applicable rules and your situation; sort that out once a month against the official guidance or with an adviser, not entry by entry.

In MoneyTag each side job is its own project with its own ledger: income minus expenses is its net, and private spending never enters. Every entry has a note for client, payment route and receipt number, plus tags such as Materials, Travel or Mixed; the Tags screen totals the same tag across all projects. For handing over, export as CSV or PDF. Data stays in on-device storage. Free to start; unlimited projects, tags and reports are a one-time purchase.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether the side job gets its own ledger, separate from the household budget
  • Whether every entry has a note field for client, payment route and receipt number
  • Whether entries can be tagged and the tags totalled across all ledgers later
  • Whether CSV or PDF export exists in case someone has to check the figures
  • Whether data stays on the device, and whether it is a one-time purchase instead of a subscription

The steps that actually work

  1. Create a ledger that holds only the side job. Private spending is never recorded there, not even temporarily.
  2. Record each payment on the day it arrives, with the amount; client and payment route go in the note.
  3. Record an expense only if it would not exist without the side job. Keep the receipt and write its number in the note.
  4. Use three fixed tags: Materials, Travel, Mixed. Do not add a fourth, or tagging quietly stops.
  5. Once a month, read the net and classify the Mixed entries against the official rules, or have them checked.

FAQ

Do I need a separate bank account for the side job?

Not necessarily for a clean record. What matters is that every side-job entry lands in its own ledger with the payment route in the note. Whether a separate account is required or advisable depends on your situation and the applicable rules.

What about expenses that are both private and work?

Tag them Mixed and do not split them on the spot. The share that counts toward the side job follows the official rules; settle it once a month rather than at every entry.
