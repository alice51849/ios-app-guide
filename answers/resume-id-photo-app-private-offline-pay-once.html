Short answer

Resume and application photo norms differ by country and employer, and some places discourage photos entirely, so first confirm that a photo is appropriate. If a formal ID-style portrait is requested, Snapport Lite can make a clean resume photo using common templates, custom sizing, background replacement, and light identity-safe adjustments such as brightness, contrast, warmth, exposure, and sharpen. It is processed on your iPhone and bought once, but it is not a beauty retoucher or a guarantee that an employer will accept a specific photo style.

Try Snapport Lite on a real example first, and check the current App Store listing for exact features and pricing before you decide.

What to look for before choosing

  • Custom size support for portals that ask for exact pixels rather than a country preset
  • Plain background replacement to remove distracting rooms, posters, or shadows
  • Light, identity-safe adjustments instead of heavy filters that can look unprofessional
  • JPEG, PNG, or PDF export depending on the job portal or document workflow
  • Private, offline, no-account processing for a sensitive career document photo

A practical decision process

  1. Define the job you need done most often.
  2. Test the app with real content or a realistic scenario.
  3. Check privacy labels and account requirements.
  4. Confirm export and backup options.
  5. Choose the pricing model you are comfortable maintaining.

FAQ

Can Snapport Lite make a resume or CV photo?

Yes. The App Store description lists resume templates and custom size creation, so you can prepare a formal ID-style photo when a job portal or regional convention asks for one.

Should every resume include a photo?

No. Resume-photo expectations vary by country, industry, and employer. Use Snapport Lite only when a photo is requested or culturally appropriate.

Does Snapport Lite change my face?

Snapport Lite offers light adjustments such as brightness, contrast, warmth, exposure, and sharpen. It is meant to keep the photo identity-safe, not to create a misleading retouched portrait.
