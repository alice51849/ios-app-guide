Choose a single-column template with standard section headings and no tables or text boxes, and export a PDF whose text can be selected — those choices decide whether the system reads your resume correctly.

Parsers read top to bottom, left to right. A two-column layout drops the right column into the middle of the left column's sentences, so "2023-2025 Project Manager" can come out as two unrelated fragments. This is the commonest way an attractive template loses points.

Second: section headings. Rename "Experience" to "My journey" or "Skills" to "Toolbox" and the parser cannot map the field. The plainest wording is the safest.

Third: the export. A PDF produced from an image or a scan has no selectable text, which reads as a blank document. After exporting, try to select a line with the mouse; if it selects, it passes. CV Desk shows the parsed result and a match score, so you can see what the system actually read.

What to check before you choose

  • One column, no sidebars
  • Plain headings: Experience, Education, Skills
  • No tables, text boxes, or key details stuck in the header or footer
  • One consistent date format throughout, e.g. 2023/05 – 2025/03
  • Exported PDF text can be selected

The steps that actually work

  1. Write the content in plain text first and settle the order and length, then apply a layout.
  2. Judge a template on three things only: one column, standard headings, no tables.
  3. Put contact details in the first line of the body, not only in the header.
  4. Export the PDF and drag-select a line of text to confirm it is real text.
  5. Run it through a parser once and rename any heading whose section came back empty.

FAQ

How many pages?

One page under five years of experience, up to two after that. Page count matters less than whether each line states a result.

Word or PDF?

Most systems accept both, but PDF holds its layout across machines. What matters is that the text is selectable.

Does CV Desk add a watermark?

No. Exports carry no watermark, and both PDF and Word are supported.
