Reward chart for a 3–7 year old: keeping it alive past week two

A child does not get bored of stars. They get bored of a chart that has not changed in three weeks while they change every day.

Every Monday change exactly one thing on the chart — the reward, the stickers, or the star threshold — and never all three at once. Stars go on right after the task, the threshold climbs from 3 to 5 to 7, and a 3–7 year old will stay with it for more than a month.

Every chart runs well in week one because the stars are new. By week two or three, with the same reward, the same stickers and the same threshold, the chart has become wallpaper. The clearest sign: the child stops asking "how many stars did I get today". That is not the moment to print a bigger chart. What needs changing is the rhythm, not the size.

The fix is rotation: week 1, threshold three stars and a small reward that costs nothing; week 2, same threshold and reward, only the stickers change (stars become hearts or cars); week 3, threshold five, and the child picks the reward from three options; week 4, stickers change again. Two things never change: the star goes on right after the task, by the child's own hand, and a star once given is never removed. Remove one and the child stops trusting the chart.

When the paper chart is torn or the family is away, the same rhythm can move to the phone. What to check before you choose

  • Whether the child can stick the star on right after the task, or has to wait until evening
  • Whether the reward can change weekly without costing money
  • Whether the threshold can rise over time, or is fixed at one number
  • Whether the chart hangs at the child's eye level, somewhere they pass daily
  • If it is an app: ads, connection needed, recurring charges

The steps that actually work

  1. Pick 4–5 tasks the child can already do but dawdles over: brushing teeth, dressing alone, tidying toys, getting ready for bed. Skip anything they cannot yet do alone.
  2. Week 1: threshold three stars, a small reward that costs nothing — choosing tonight's story, ten extra minutes playing with a parent. The child sticks the star on right after the task.
  3. Week 2: keep the threshold and reward, change only the stickers or the colour of the chart.
  4. Week 3: raise the threshold to five, write down three rewards and let the child point to one; write it at the top of the chart.
  5. From week 4: change exactly one thing every Monday. Any week the child does not mention stars, change the reward the following week instead of waiting for the month to end.

FAQ

What if the child wants a reward after every single star?

Normal at this age. Keep the three-star threshold in week one, but pair each star with one specific line of praise: "you got your shirt on by yourself". Praise is the instant reward; the treat is the threshold reward.

Should I remove a star when the child misbehaves?

No. The chart only adds, never subtracts. Remove one star and the child stops trusting the chart. A task not done is an empty cell, with nothing said.

Does Lumi Mission Planet charge monthly?

No. The app is free to download, a one-time unlock opens the full set, there is no recurring charge and no ads.
