Days 1 and 2: use the built-in Screen Time report to find your top three apps and daily pickups. Days 3 and 4: lock one 25–50 minute block a day. Days 5 and 6: add the hour before bed and the half hour after waking. Day 7: compare the numbers and keep only the rules that worked.

Days 1 and 2 are for measuring, nothing else. Open Settings, then Screen Time, and write down your top three apps and how many times a day you pick the phone up. Most people find the time is going to one or two apps, not five. Change no habits yet; the point is knowing which app you are up against.

Days 3 and 4 introduce the timed block. Pick a 25–50 minute stretch you already wanted to protect, say after lunch or at eight in the evening, lock the top three apps for that stretch, and let them unlock on their own when it ends. The word that matters is block, not forever: a brain that knows the feed comes back in an hour does not fight the lock. Days 5 and 6 add the hour before bed and the thirty minutes after waking, the two windows where phone time grows fastest.

On day 7, go back to the numbers. Did pickups fall, did the top three lose minutes? Keep the rules that moved a number, drop the rest, add one more next week. What to check before you choose

  • Whether you know your daily pickups and the minutes on your top three apps
  • Whether a lock has a clear end time and unlocks on its own
  • Whether stopping early has friction, such as a cooldown and a typed confirmation
  • Whether bedtime and morning have their own modes instead of manual setup each night
  • Whether there is a weekly chart so day 7 shows a change

The steps that actually work

  1. Days 1–2: in Settings, open Screen Time and note your top three apps and daily pickups. Change nothing yet.
  2. Days 3–4: lock the top three apps for one 25–50 minute block a day and let the timer unlock them.
  3. Day 5: add a lock for the hour before bed and charge the phone somewhere other than the bed.
  4. Day 6: add thirty phone-free minutes after waking; do one planned thing before the apps come back.
  5. Day 7: compare with day 1, keep the blocks that moved a number, and add one more rule next week.

FAQ

Do I have to delete my social apps?

No, and most people who do reinstall them within three days. Locking a fixed block is easier to sustain for a week because you know the apps come back when the timer ends, so nothing feels taken away.

What if I still want to unlock on day 3?

Shorten the block to 25 minutes, get through it, then lengthen it slowly. If a single tap to unlock is too easy, turn on Hard Mode: stopping early then needs a cooldown and a typed confirmation, which turns the unlock into a deliberate decision.
