Sticker chart: how to make one, and the rules that stop it dying in week three

A sticker chart does not fail because the child lacks motivation. It fails on the number of rows and the moment the sticker goes on.

Five rows at most, the child sticks the sticker on the moment the task is done, and the first reward comes at three stickers. Keep those three and a 3–7 year old carries on past week three.

The chart is five rows down and seven days across. Rows are actions, not "be good": brush teeth, finish dinner, tidy up, bath, bed. Past five rows the child can no longer see "nearly there" and more of them quit halfway. It goes low on the fridge, at the child's eye level. A chart hung at adult height does not exist to a child.

Running it comes down to four rules. One: the sticker goes on right after the task, by the child's hand. Stuck on at night in a batch, a three year old cannot connect it to the morning teeth. Two: a sticker once on is never peeled off. A chart that can lose stickers becomes a punishment and the child stops looking at it. Three: rewards are experiences rather than things, and the bar rises in steps — three stickers at first, five from week two, a full row from week three. Four: every Monday change one thing only, the sticker design or the reward, never both.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether there are five rows or fewer, and every row is an action
  • Whether it hangs low enough for the child to stick the sticker on themselves right away
  • Whether the first reward arrives at about three stickers
  • Whether peeling stickers off has become a punishment
  • If it is an app: ads, accounts and subscriptions

The steps that actually work

  1. Write down the moments that cause a daily fight, then keep only five actions the child can do alone.
  2. Make a five-by-seven grid and hang it low on the fridge at the child's eye level, with the stickers right next to it.
  3. Week one: the child sticks the sticker on right after the task; three stickers earn a small reward. Decide the reward in advance and write it under the chart.
  4. Week two: raise it to five stickers and offer the reward as a choice of three. Week three: one reward per full row.
  5. From week three on: every Monday change either the sticker design or the reward, never both. If one row has few stickers, split that one row into two shorter actions.

FAQ

What makes a good reward?

Experiences over things: choosing tonight's picture book, ten minutes of play together, picking the bath toy. Something you can hand out every two or three days without getting tired of it.

Do I peel a sticker off on a bad day?

No. A chart that can lose stickers becomes a punishment and the child stops looking at it. A missed day stays blank, and nothing is said.

Is Lumi Mission Planet a subscription?
