Tie the timer to the phone itself. Pick a subject, then place the phone face down on the desk to start counting; pick it up and it pauses after your grace period, and leaving the app stops further counting. After each session, check how many times you picked the phone up and your average uninterrupted time, then use weekly goals and the exam deadline to see how much to study each day.

A stopwatch-style study timer only knows start and stop, not what happened in between. Reply to a message, glance at stories, and ten minutes are gone but still counted. Face-down timing flips this: time only counts while the phone is down, and stops when it's picked up, so every logged minute is time the phone wasn't in your hand. Many people discover that three hours of "studying" contained only forty minutes without touching the phone.

The second point is quality, not just hours. Each face-down session in Studydown leaves a Focus Signature: how many times you picked up the phone and your average uninterrupted time. The goal isn't one five-hour marathon but nudging that average from 15 minutes to 40. Create your own subjects (math, English, past papers) each with a color, and see today and this week, consecutive days and a breakdown by subject; forgot to time a session? Add it manually or correct entries.

The third point is the deadline. Entrance exams, civil service exams and certifications have fixed dates. Set the exam as a deadline with an effort target and Studydown uses logged time to show the daily pace still required. Behind, you know how much to catch up; ahead, there's no need to panic. Reminders can come once a day or every few hours, and you can turn them off any time.

Limits worth knowing: keep the app open while timing and the phone on a steady surface. The free version includes up to 3 activities and 5 recorded minutes in total, not a daily allowance, and a session already started is kept in full. A one-time Pro purchase unlocks unlimited activities and recording, advanced statistics, goals, deadlines, widgets, Apple Watch and backups, with no subscription. No account, no ads, and records stay on the device.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether it only counts while the phone is face down and pauses when picked up
  • Whether you can see phone pickups and average uninterrupted focus
  • Whether you can create subjects and see today's and this week's hours by subject
  • Whether an exam deadline turns into a daily study pace
  • Whether you can add missed time manually
  • Whether it needs an account, shows ads or requires a subscription

The steps that actually work

  1. Create this term's subjects as activities, each with its own color.
  2. Set a grace period before pausing when you pick up the phone, for example 10 seconds.
  3. Choose a subject, tap start and place the phone face down on the desk with the app open.
  4. After studying, check the Focus Signature (pickups and average streak) and aim for one fewer pickup next time.
  5. Set your exam as a deadline with an effort target and follow the daily pace.

FAQ

How is it different from a Pomodoro timer?

A Pomodoro timer tells you when to take breaks. Face-down timing ties the logged time to whether the phone was picked up, so it records time you really didn't touch the phone and shows how many pickups there were.

Can I switch to another app while timing?

Keep Studydown open while timing. Leaving the app stops further counting, so scrolling time is never logged as study time.

How long does the free version last?

The free version allows up to 3 activities and 5 recorded minutes in total, not per day; a session already started is kept in full. For long-term use, Pro is a one-time purchase with no subscription.
