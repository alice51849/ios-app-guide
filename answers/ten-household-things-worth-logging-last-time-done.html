Log whatever repeats in weeks or months and leaves no visible trace: sheets, the air-conditioner filter, flea treatment, photo backup, the water-filter cartridge, deworming, pillows, the fridge, the water tank and the car's oil. Count every interval from the day you actually did it, not the day it was scheduled.

The four most frequent run on a cycle of weeks. Sheets every 1 to 2 weeks, closer to 1 with a pet on the bed or hot weather; the air-conditioner filter washed every 2 to 4 weeks when the unit runs daily. Flea treatment monthly, on the day the packet says; phone photo backup once a month and before any trip. These slip unnoticed because the house gives no sign that the interval has passed.

The other six take months, which is exactly why they vanish from memory. Water-filter cartridge or purifier candle every 6 months, sooner if the flow drops; deworming every 3 to 6 months on the vet's schedule; pillows washed every 3 to 6 months and replaced when they no longer spring back. Fridge fully cleaned with a seal check every 1 to 3 months; the rooftop water tank cleaned every 6 months; the car's oil at whichever comes first, the manual's mileage or 12 months, because oil ages even when the car sits. For all of them the clock starts on the day it was done, not the day it was due.

The usual mistake is putting these on the calendar. The calendar fires on the 15th even though you changed the filter on the 10th, so the next alert is already wrong. What works is logging the completion and letting the next due date restart from there: Mochi DoneStamp does exactly that, one tap stores the exact time, with an approximate or exact rhythm from minutes to years, local reminders with Done and Snooze, and Home and Lock Screen widgets. What to check before you choose

  • Whether the item leaves any visible sign that it is overdue (sheets yes, dewormer no)
  • Whether the interval is weeks or months: the longer it is, the more a log is worth
  • Whether the date that matters is the real completion, not the calendar entry
  • Whether someone else in the house also does this task and needs to see the log
  • Whether the tool lets you correct the date when you logged it late

The steps that actually work

  1. Walk through the house and list everything that repeats monthly or slower and leaves no trace: filter, water tank, dewormer, oil.
  2. For each item, write down the last time you remember, even roughly; 'around June' beats a blank.
  3. Set each item's frequency from the numbers above and adjust for your home: pets, kids, a hot city.
  4. When you do the task, log it on the spot, before the bucket or the packet goes away; that is where memory is lost.
  5. Once a month, open the list and look only at what comes due in the next few weeks; the rest can stay quiet.

FAQ

Why not just use the phone's calendar?

Because the calendar repeats on a fixed date and life does not. Change the filter five days before the alert and the next alert is already five days off; after three changes the error is measured in weeks.

What if I forgot to log it at the time?

Log it as soon as you remember, with an approximate date. 'Around the 10th' is worth far more than nothing, and you can correct it later if the receipt or a photo turns up.
