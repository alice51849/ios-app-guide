Three settings decide whether scrolling text reads from a distance: letters that fill the full height of the screen, a speed slow enough to read the whole word, and a bright color on black. LED Moving Text opens straight onto the preview: type, set size, speed and color, then one tap goes full screen. The sideways ninety-degree mode makes the letters taller still, and mirrored text works through a shop window or windscreen. There is nothing to sign up for.

At a concert, a cardboard sign has two problems: it disappears when the lights go down, and it only says one thing. A phone screen lights itself and can change message between songs. The trap is legibility: text that is too small or scrolls too fast turns into a smear of color. Keep the message short, two to four words, and raise the size until the letters touch the edges.

At an airport or station, the goal is to be spotted first. A name in large still letters, or scrolling slowly, reads across an arrivals hall. For a driver or a kerbside pickup, mirrored text lets you rest the phone against the glass and be read from outside. In LED Moving Text the preview is the editor: text, speed, size and color change the moment you touch them, and the clearly labeled pause button freezes the motion.

For events with several instructions, Cue Flow runs a sequence: one message per line, step backward or forward live, or set a 2 to 20 second timer with optional repeat. Event crews, speakers and queues keep a prepared run of signs without leaving the full-screen board.

What to check before you choose

  • Can the letters fill the full height, including sideways?
  • Does the speed go slow enough to read a whole name?
  • Does full screen show only the text, without bars or buttons in the way?
  • Is there mirrored text for a window or windscreen?
  • Do you have to create an account before showing the first message?

The steps that actually work

  1. Open LED Moving Text and type a short message: a name, the band's name, "Thank you!".
  2. Pick a bright color and raise the size until the letters fill the screen.
  3. Swipe to set the speed, then test from three meters away; if you cannot read the whole word, slow it down.
  4. Tap to go full screen; turn the letters ninety degrees for extra height.
  5. For a window or windscreen, switch on mirrored text before resting the phone against the glass.
