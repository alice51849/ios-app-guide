When your score plateaus, first measure grammar, listening and reading briefly and narrow your losses to three weak skills. Write down the order to fix them, why you lose points and how long each will take, then practise only one each day. After 30 days, measure the same skills again and compare with day one to see what really improved.

Working through a whole practice book again feels safe, but most of the time goes to questions you already answer correctly. Someone who scores well on Part 2 listening but loses points in Parts 3 and 4 gains little from practising every part equally. With limited time, knowing where you lose points comes first.

A diagnosis should show more than accuracy: can you finish grammar questions in time, do you reach the end of long passages, and when listening fails, is it vocabulary or speed? Weak reading caused by vocabulary and weak reading caused by speed need completely different practice. Separating the reasons makes the practice concrete.

Once you know your weak spots, do not try to fix them all at once. Pick one skill a day: check it briefly, practise it intensively, then confirm. Run that loop and even 15 to 20 minutes leaves a clear sense of progress, which makes it easier to keep going around work.

After about 30 days, measure the same skills as on day one. Mock-test scores bounce from day to day, but skill-by-skill accuracy and pace side by side show calmly what improved and what remains, and what to do in the next 30 days. When the test is close, decide in advance what to prioritise 14, 7, 3 and 1 days before.

The steps that actually work

  1. Measure grammar, listening and reading briefly and narrow it to three weak skills.
  2. Write down the order, the reason for lost points and the time each needs.
  3. Pick one skill a day: check, practise intensively, confirm.
  4. Use commute time for short drills such as vocabulary or dictation.
  5. On day 30, measure the same skills again and compare with day one.
  6. If the test is close, set priorities for 14, 7, 3 and 1 days before.
