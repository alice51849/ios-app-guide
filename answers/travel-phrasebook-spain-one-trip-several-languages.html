Phrasebook: one trip, several languages

On a rail pass or a multi-city trip, the language at the station, the hotel and the restaurant changes every few days. Carrying a different phrasebook for each country rarely works.

For a trip with several languages, prepare the same four or five day-one situations in each (station or airport, hotel, asking directions, ordering food), rehearse each line together with the reply you are likely to hear, and review the next destination's language a day or two before you arrive.

Trying to learn three languages at once before leaving usually ends in mixed-up phrases. It works better to fix the same situations for all of them: if you can buy a ticket, check in and ask for a street in Italian, do the same in French and Portuguese. The list is short and repeats in every country.

The hard part is not the line you say but what comes back. At hotel reception you may be asked for your passport or arrival time; at the station, whether you want a return. Rehearsing the line with the likely reply prepares you for the second half of the exchange, which is where most people freeze.

DailyMate Lite has 50 languages and each keeps its own progress and saved phrases, so you can switch from Italian to French without losing anything. It covers 84 real situations, each card pairing a line you will hear with a natural reply, and pronunciation uses on-device voices at adjustable speed. The Travel topic at beginner level is free in any language.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether each language keeps its progress separately
  • Whether phrases come with the reply you will hear
  • Whether you can hear pronunciation and slow it down
  • Whether the situations match the trip: station, hotel, food
  • Whether you can review a few minutes a day without a book

The steps that actually work

  1. Write down the order of countries and each one's language.
  2. Pick the same four or five day-one situations for all of them.
  3. Start with the first destination's language, line and reply together.
  4. A day or two before changing country, review the next language.
  5. Save the phrases you actually used to favourites and review them.
