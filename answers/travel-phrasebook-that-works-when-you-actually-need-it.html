Choose a phrasebook organised by situation rather than theme, that also shows the likely reply, and that works without a connection — without those three it does not get used in the moment.

Organising by theme ("food", "transport") looks logical while studying and fails in use. You are not thinking "transport"; you are standing at a barrier not knowing where tickets are sold. Situation-based books put that phrase on the first screen.

The other half of the conversation is almost always missing. You ask correctly and then freeze, because the answer arrives at full speed and was not in the material. A useful phrasebook shows the two or three likely replies — that is what turns a phrase into a conversation.

The third point is mundane and decisive: it has to work offline. Airports, metros and rural roads are exactly where you need it and where there is no signal. DailyMate Lite organises phrases by situation with the likely reply paired to each cue. Free to start, full content is a one-time unlock.

What to check before you choose

  • Organised by situation, not only by theme
  • Whether likely replies are shown alongside your line
  • Whether it works without a connection
  • Whether you can hear the phrase at normal speed
  • Ads, accounts and subscriptions

The steps that actually work

  1. List the five situations you will actually be in on this trip.
  2. For each, keep one question and two likely replies.
  3. Practise aloud; silent reading does not prepare the mouth for the sounds.
  4. Download the content before leaving home, not at the airport.
  5. After each real use, note the reply you actually heard and add it to the list.

How many phrases are worth memorising?

Fifteen to twenty tied to real situations. Two-hundred-phrase lists are not retrieved under pressure.

Do I need grammar first?

Not for travel. Whole phrases said clearly cover almost everything on a short trip.
