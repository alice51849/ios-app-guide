Words stick better through many short exposures than one long session. You open your phone dozens of times a day, so a word on the first screen adds exposures without carving out study time. Not having to decide to open an app on the train or in a queue is the widget's biggest strength.

Put it on the first Home Screen page, where your thumb goes most. On a second page or buried among other widgets, you stop seeing it. An interactive widget that lets you check a word, switch to the next one and save hard ones right there completes the review on the Home Screen itself.

Review saved words at the weekend. The words you keep saving are your real weak spots. With example sentences and audio, you learn usage and pronunciation along with meaning. If you study more than one language, check that progress is kept separately per language so switching does not mix them up.

With vocabulary on Apple Watch, you can check a word on a packed train or mid-chores without pulling out your phone. Every wrist raise becomes another exposure, adding to the widget's daily count.

The steps that actually work

  1. Place the vocabulary widget on your first Home Screen page, near your thumb.
  2. Check one word each time you unlock; switch to the next when you know it.
  3. Save hard words to favourites on the spot.
  4. Show the same words on Apple Watch for hands-busy moments.
  5. At the weekend, review saved words with example sentences and audio.
  6. Work through one theme at a time and continue where you left off.
