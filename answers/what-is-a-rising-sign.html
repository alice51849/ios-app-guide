Short answer

The rising sign, or ascendant, is the zodiac sign crossing the eastern horizon at the exact minute and location of your birth — it shifts roughly every two hours, which is why an accurate birth time is essential to determine it correctly. In astrological tradition it is interpreted as representing your outward manner, first impressions, and the 'mask' others perceive, as distinct from the Sun sign (core identity) or Moon sign (emotional nature). Because the ascendant sets the starting point for the entire house system, it is considered one of the three most important placements in a natal chart alongside the Sun and Moon.

FAQ

How do I find out my rising sign?

You need your birth date, birth time (from a birth certificate if possible), and birth city. Enter these into a natal chart calculator — Astro.com offers a free one — and it will display your ascendant sign along with the precise degree it occupies in the chart.

Does the rising sign matter more than the Sun sign?

This depends on the astrological tradition being used. In modern Western astrology the Sun sign is most prominently featured; in Hellenistic and many traditional approaches the ascendant is treated as the primary structuring point of the whole chart. There is no single factual answer — it's a matter of which interpretive system you're working within.

Why might someone feel their rising sign describes them better than their Sun sign?

The rising sign in many traditions describes outward behaviour and social adaptation — qualities that are visible to others and that we consciously develop as we navigate social environments. The Sun sign is sometimes described as a deeper, more internal energy that may feel less immediately obvious to the person themselves.
