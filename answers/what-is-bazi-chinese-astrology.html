Short answer

BaZi (八字, 'Eight Characters'), also called the Four Pillars of Destiny, is a traditional Chinese system that derives eight characters from a person's birth year, month, day, and hour using the Chinese lunisolar calendar. Each of the four pillars pairs a Heavenly Stem and an Earthly Branch, and all elements map to the Five Elements theory (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water) in yin and yang forms; the interactions among these elements are interpreted as reflecting personality tendencies and life patterns. Like all astrological systems, BaZi is best approached as a framework for self-reflection and cultural interest — it is a rich and intricate tradition, but its predictive claims are not scientifically verified.

FAQ

Is BaZi the same as the Chinese zodiac (Year of the Rat, Dragon, etc.)?

No — the popular Chinese zodiac uses only the birth year (a 12-year animal cycle and a straightforward lookup). BaZi uses all four time pillars (year, month, day, hour), producing vastly more individual combinations. The year animal sign appears within BaZi as one of eight characters; the full system is considerably more individualised than the commonly known year-sign alone.

How is BaZi different from Western astrology?

Both use birth timing to construct a symbolic profile, but they originate from entirely different cosmological traditions. Western astrology focuses on planetary positions against the zodiacal ecliptic; BaZi focuses on the Five Elements balance derived from the Chinese stems-and-branches calendar. They are independent systems and cannot be meaningfully translated into each other.

Do I need a special calendar conversion to read my BaZi chart?

Yes — BaZi uses the Chinese Solar Calendar (Hsia Calendar, or 干支 stems-and-branches system), not the Gregorian calendar directly. Birth dates near Chinese solar term boundaries (節, occurring roughly every 15 days) can fall in a different month pillar than expected, so dedicated BaZi software or a practitioner familiar with that calendar system is needed for accuracy.
