Rule of thumb: above 25 degrees one layer, 15 to 25 two, 5 to 15 three, below 5 three to four layers plus hat and gloves. Work from the feels-like temperature, not the air temperature, and let the neck test after ten minutes decide whether a layer comes off.

Layering only works when each layer has a job: the inner layer (a cotton or wool-silk bodysuit or vest) takes up sweat, the middle (jumper, fleece, cardigan) warms, the outer (jacket, rain trousers, snowsuit) keeps out wind and wet. Two thin layers are warmer than one thick one because the air between them insulates, and one can come off on the way without leaving the child in a T-shirt.

The bands for a toddler who moves on their own: above 25 degrees, shorts and a sun hat, a UV shirt near water. 20 to 25, a thin long sleeve or T-shirt with a light jacket in the buggy. 15 to 20, long sleeve plus a light jacket or gilet. 10 to 15, bodysuit, jumper, transitional jacket, a thin hat if windy. 5 to 10, vest, jumper, winter coat, hat, gloves. 0 to 5, thermal base layer, fleece, winter coat or snowsuit, scarf. Below 0, snowsuit, wool socks, a footmuff in the buggy, and back inside after 30 to 45 minutes. Babies who are carried or lying down get one layer more than the chart; children who run get one less.

The mistake is rarely the chart. It is reading the thermometer: wind, wet and missing sun take several degrees off. What to check before you choose

  • Feels-like temperature for the hour you will be outside, not the day's high
  • Wind above 20 km/h: add a layer or move the windproof layer to the outside
  • Rain chance in the next hour, because wet cools faster than cold
  • Whether the child is moving, or sitting in a buggy or carrier
  • Whether there is somewhere to take a layer off and stow it on the way

The steps that actually work

  1. In the evening open the hourly forecast for the planned time outside and note the feels-like temperature, not the air temperature.
  2. Lay out the layers for that band: inner takes sweat, middle warms, outer blocks wind and wet.
  3. Add a layer for buggy or carrier; take one off for a child who runs and climbs.
  4. Ten minutes after going out, do the neck test: warm and dry is right, damp means a layer off, cool means add a hat or jacket. Hands and feet often feel colder than the child is.
  5. The removed layer goes in the buggy or backpack, not tied around the child; it is needed again on the way home as evening cools.

FAQ

Does the chart apply to babies under one?

As a base, yes, but with one extra layer, because babies do not move and hold temperature less well. In a footmuff or carrier the carrier counts as a layer, so no thick coat underneath.

How do I tell the child is too warm?

Damp neck, red face, fussing for no reason. Take the middle layer off at once and offer a drink. Cold hands on their own are not a sign of being cold.
