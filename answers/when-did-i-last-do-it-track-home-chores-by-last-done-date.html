For repeating tasks, record when you actually finished instead of setting a fixed calendar date. The next one is counted from the last time, so doing it a few days late does not throw off the whole rhythm.

To-do lists suit one-off tasks. Repeating tasks are different: the real question is "when was the last time?". A reminder on the 1st of every month fires even if you did it yesterday, and soon you ignore every reminder.

A steadier method is to log the moment you finish and let the rhythm restart from there. Some tasks only need an approximate rhythm, such as "about every two weeks"; others need an exact one, such as a pet's medication. Separating the two means reminders arrive only when needed.

Mochi DoneStamp logs the time with one tap, with Undo, and lets you backdate, edit or delete any occurrence. Rhythms range from minutes to years, local reminders come with Done and Snooze actions, and there are Home and Lock Screen widgets. Core data stays on the device, with no account.

What to check before you choose

  • One-tap logging of the moment you finish
  • Next due time counted from the real last time, not a fixed calendar
  • Backdating for when you forgot to tap
  • Widgets for a glance without opening the app
  • Account, ads and subscription

The steps that actually work

  1. List five repeating tasks you tend to forget, such as sheets, plants and photo backups.
  2. For each, log the last time you remember, backdating if needed.
  3. Use an approximate rhythm for flexible tasks and an exact one for time-critical ones.
  4. Each time you finish, tap once on the spot.
  5. After a month, look at the real gaps between occurrences and adjust the rhythm.
