"Connected but no internet" usually means a problem at one of four places: the router, DNS, the provider's line, or one particular site. Finding where it breaks is faster than restarting by guesswork. WiFi Aid Lite checks Wi-Fi, DNS and the internet in one tap, names the most likely cause, and shows the evidence behind it: DNS, TCP, TLS, TTFB, HTTP, direct IP, IPv4 and IPv6. There is no account, ads or tracking, and records stay on the device.

Full Wi-Fi bars only mean the wireless link between phone and router is alive. Behind it, the router may have lost the internet line, DNS may not be answering, or the provider may have an outage. Or the internet is fine and only one site you use is failing. Where the fault sits decides whether to restart the router, look at DNS settings, or call your provider.

WiFi Aid Lite runs independent path, DNS, TCP, TLS, timing and stability checks and compares the evidence to reach a conclusion. Check a Website tells you whether one destination or the wider internet is failing, and Deep Check collects more evidence and stability samples to catch problems that come and go. Variation analysis spots a connection that is up but keeps dropping.

The result helps when you call support too. "DNS answers but the TLS connection fails" gets further than "the internet is broken". Check History keeps past results so you can compare times of day, and records are stored only on this device.

What to check before you choose

  • Does it check Wi-Fi, DNS and the internet separately in one go?
  • Does it show evidence for each item, not just a verdict?
  • Can it tell whether only one site is down?
  • Does it catch an unstable connection that drops on and off?
  • Does it work without an account and keep records on the device?

The steps that actually work

  1. While connected to Wi-Fi, open WiFi Aid Lite and tap Run a Check.
  2. Read the most likely cause and the item-by-item results for DNS, TCP, TLS and more.
  3. If only one app or site fails, use Check a Website on that address.
  4. If it comes and goes, run Deep Check to gather more stability samples.
  5. If the router is to blame, restart it; if the line is, call your provider with the result in front of you.

FAQ

Won't restarting the router fix it?

Only when the router is the cause. If DNS or the provider's line is at fault, repeated restarts change nothing, so find where it breaks first.

Can it tell if only one site is down?

Yes, Check a Website shows whether one destination or the wider internet is failing.

Are my check results sent anywhere?

No. Records are stored only on this device.
