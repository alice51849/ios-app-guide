Structure the slide as one headline sentence (your biggest contribution this year), three results each with a before-and-after number, and one bottom line naming next year's priority and the support you need. Everything else goes in an appendix.

The hard part is cutting, not layout. List everything you did, then filter with one question: "If I hadn't done this, what would the team have lost?" Keep only items with a concrete answer — usually five to eight — and pick the three that best show your role this year. Maintenance and helping others matter, but unless there's a measurable result, mention them verbally or in the appendix.

Every result needs a before and after. "Improved reporting" means little; "monthly report cut from three days to half a day" is clear. Make sure every number traces back to a source, because reviewers ask. Without a number, use concrete scope: "took over 4 accounts, all renewed". The last line — next year's priority and what you need — is often what the review conversation actually turns on.

If your raw material is a year of weekly updates, meeting notes or spreadsheets, OnePage PPT can draft the first version: type, paste, snap a photo or import a PDF or spreadsheet, tap Make My Slide, and the content is summarised into headlines and turned into the right visual — numbers into charts, dates into a timeline — and it only draws a chart when the data supports it. Export an editable .pptx, a PDF or a high-resolution PNG.

What to check before you choose

  • One headline sentence at the top
  • Only three results
  • A before-and-after number or concrete scope for each
  • Numbers that trace back to a source
  • A bottom line with next year's priority and the support you need

The steps that actually work

  1. Pull out this year's weekly updates, project lists and goals; list everything you did.
  2. Filter with "what would we have lost?" and keep three.
  3. Add a before-and-after number to each, with its source noted.
  4. Write the headline sentence and the bottom line for next year.
  5. Ask a colleague to read it in 30 seconds; cut whatever they can't repeat back.

FAQ

Is one slide really enough for a year-end review?

For the page your manager reads first, yes. Put details and evidence in an appendix you open when asked.

How do I write up work without impressive numbers?

Use concrete scope or outcomes, such as "supported 3 teams through a system rollout". Specific counts beat adjectives.

Can I edit OnePage PPT's export?

Yes. The .pptx is editable in PowerPoint, Keynote and Google Slides.
