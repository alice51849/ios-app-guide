FAQ

Should I type notes or write them by hand?

Mueller & Oppenheimer (2014, Psychological Science) found handwritten notes produced better conceptual understanding because limited writing speed forces summarisation over transcription. For meetings where notes need to be shared or searched later, typed notes in a consistent format are more practical — the best choice depends on whether retention or searchability is your priority.

Is recording a meeting audio useful as a substitute for note-taking?

Recording is an excellent safety net for complex or fast-moving meetings, but passive re-listening is inefficient. The recording is most valuable for verifying a specific decision or exact wording rather than as a replacement for real-time capture. Even minimal live notes alongside a recording leads to far better action-item follow-through than recording alone.

How do I handle a meeting where the agenda changes or everything moves too fast to capture?

Prioritise decisions and owners over everything else — those are the outputs that drive work forward. If pace is too fast, jot keywords and timestamps, then fill gaps from a recording immediately after. A concise list of decisions and a complete action-item list is more useful than dense, incomplete paragraphs.
