Fertility and fertile days: how cycle apps estimate them, and their limits

A fertile-day calendar looks precise, but behind it is a calculation from your past cycles. It helps to know what it is based on.

The app estimates ovulation from the length of your previous cycles and marks a fertile window of roughly six days around it. With a regular cycle the estimate is useful for planning, but it is not contraception or a medical diagnosis.

Ovulation usually happens about two weeks before the next period, not necessarily mid-cycle. That is why it is worth logging the real start of each period: the estimate only sharpens after several cycles.

If your cycles are irregular, or after childbirth, while breastfeeding or with a change in medication, a calendar estimate is less reliable. In those cases it is wise to talk to a doctor rather than rely on an app alone.

Cyca logs your period, symptoms, mood and notes in a tap and learns your rhythm. Predictions for the next period, fertile window and ovulation adapt with every entry. Data stays on your iPhone, with no account, cloud or tracking, and the app works fully offline.

What to check before you choose

  • An estimate that adapts to your logged cycles
  • Clear wording that it is an estimate, not contraception
  • Where sensitive health data is stored
  • Logging fast enough to keep up every month
  • Account, ads and subscription

The steps that actually work

  1. Log the first day of each period as soon as it starts.
  2. Add symptoms and mood too, so your own patterns appear.
  3. After three to six cycles, compare the estimate with what happened.
  4. With irregular cycles, treat the fertile window as a rough guide only.
  5. If you are trying to conceive or have concerns, talk to a doctor.
