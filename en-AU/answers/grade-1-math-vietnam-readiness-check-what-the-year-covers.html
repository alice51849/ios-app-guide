Grade 1 math: a quick check of whether your child is ready

Many parents worry their child will fall behind in grade 1 math and hand over stacks of worksheets. What matters more is knowing where the gaps are. A short check at home gives a clearer answer than ten pages of exercises.

Grade 1 math centres on numbers to 100, addition and subtraction, comparing numbers, recognising shapes and telling the time. Before starting, a child only needs three solid foundations: counting objects correctly, knowing which number is bigger, and understanding that a number splits into two parts (5 is 2 and 3). If your child can do these three things, the rest is learned in class.

Grade 1 does not require fast calculation. What the teacher needs is a child who understands how many objects a number stands for. A child who recites 1 to 100 but counts five sweets as six is not ready yet; a child who counts slowly but accurately catches up quickly.

The second foundation is comparing: looking at two groups and saying which has more, then moving on to comparing two numbers. The third is splitting and combining: knowing that 5 can be 1 and 4, or 2 and 3. This is the basis of grade 1 addition and subtraction, so a child who understands it does not need to memorise addition tables.

Check with real objects at home, no pen or paper needed: spoons, beans, toys. Ask each question once, without hints, and note where your child hesitates. Those hesitations are what to practise in the weeks before school starts.

The steps that actually work

  1. Put 7 beans on the table and ask your child to count them, pointing at each.
  2. Make two groups of 4 and 6 spoons and ask which group has more.
  3. Write 8 and 5 and ask which number is bigger.
  4. Give your child 5 toys and ask how many ways they can split them between two hands.
  5. Say any number up to 20 and ask your child to take exactly that many objects.
  6. Ask what time it is on an analogue clock showing a full hour.
  7. Note where your child hesitates and practise that a little every day.

FAQ

Is it a problem if my child has not memorised addition facts?

No. Grade 1 builds addition and subtraction on splitting and combining; understanding that 5 is 2 and 3 matters more than memorising.

Should my child learn the grade 1 curriculum in advance?

There is no need to learn the whole curriculum ahead. Check and fill three foundations: counting, comparing, splitting and combining.

How can a child who cannot read practise math?

Use real objects and picture questions read aloud, so the child focuses on numbers rather than being held back by words.

How long until my child is ready?

Every child differs. Practise the hesitant areas briefly each day, then check again after a few weeks.
