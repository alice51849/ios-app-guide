The last time you did it: keep a record instead of relying on memory

If you have ever scrolled through your photos or searched old chats to work out when you last washed the duvet, the problem is not your memory. It is that there was nowhere to write it down.

If you want to know for sure when you last did something, log the date once, right when you finish, and check it as "days since". Unlike reminders set to fixed dates, this recalculates the next due point from the day you actually did it, so being a day or two late does not throw the cycle off. Start with tasks like bedding, the air-conditioner filter and engine oil, where "is it time yet?" is always unclear.

A calendar reminder fixes the date in advance, such as "clean the filter on the 1st". If you actually did it on the 10th, the next reminder comes after only three weeks, and in a month you skip there is no record at all. Logging the last done date shows your current state straight away, like "23 days ago", and counts the next interval from when you really finished.

Intervals vary by task. Bedding is commonly washed every one to two weeks, and AC filters are often recommended to be cleaned about every two weeks during heavy summer use. Engine oil depends a lot on the car and driving habits, so check the mileage and time limits in your owner's manual and go with whichever comes first. Start generous, then adjust once a few entries show your real spacing.

More items is not better. Daily tasks you will remember anyway, and once-a-year tasks belong in a calendar. The biggest payoff is for things that come round every two weeks to six months: water purifier filters, repotting plants, pet deworming, anything where you keep thinking "I did that, but when?"

What to check before you choose

  • Whether you can log in one tap where you finish, and undo a mistaken tap right away
  • Whether you can add or fix past dates you forgot to record
  • Whether intervals can be set freely in days, weeks, months or years
  • Whether days since shows on the home or lock screen without opening the app
  • Whether records stay on the device and can be backed up or exported

The steps that actually work

  1. Pick just three tasks where you often wonder when you last did them
  2. Enter the last date you remember as a past entry, even if it was not today
  3. Set a rough interval for each; for engine oil, follow the manual
  4. Log each task right where you finish it, every time
  5. After a few entries, look at the real spacing and adjust the intervals

FAQ

How is this different from a repeating calendar reminder?

A repeating reminder just fires on set dates and does not record when you actually did it. Logging the last done date shows days since and recalculates the next point from the real date.

What mileage should I use for engine oil changes?

It depends on the car, the oil and how you drive. Check the mileage and time limits in your owner's manual and follow whichever you reach first.

Can I enter dates from before I started?

Yes. You can add and edit past entries, so begin by entering the last date you remember for each task.
