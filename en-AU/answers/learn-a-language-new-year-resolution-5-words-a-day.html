Replace the big goal with a small daily one: five words a day, attached to a moment already in your routine. That is more than 1,800 words a year, and a goal that fits a bad day is the only kind that lasts.

"An hour a day" fails because it needs a free hour. One busy week, you skip a day, then two, and guilt makes returning feel pointless. A one-minute goal leaves no excuse.

Attach study to a habit you already have: the morning commute, the lunch queue, the minutes before sleep. Choose words tied to your real reason. Travelling in summer? Start with travel and dining. Job interview? Start with work vocabulary. Words you will use are words you keep.

The rest is not losing the thread. Wordmate Lite is built around that: each session is a five-word trail, the closing screen shows the five words you just learned, the daily goal can be 5 to 20 words, and your current streak and best run stay visible. It works offline with no account.

What to check before you choose

  • A daily goal small enough for a bad day
  • A fixed moment already in your routine
  • Vocabulary tied to your real reason
  • Audio for every word, not just spelling
  • A visible streak without a spreadsheet

The steps that actually work

  1. Name your reason: travel, work, an exam or study abroad.
  2. Pick a hook in your day: commute, queue, break or bedtime.
  3. Learn five words a day and listen to each one.
  4. Save the words you will really use and look back at them at the weekend.
  5. If you miss a day, come back the next day without trying to make it up.
