Why logging the last time beats a reminder: chores, car, health

A reminder only knows the date arrived. It has no idea whether you did the thing. The real problem is rarely forgetting to do it; it is not knowing when you last did.

Reminders suit things with a hard deadline, such as car tax or a doctor's appointment. For anything that can happen any day but needs to recur, such as changing sheets, cleaning the air conditioner or an oil change, log when you last did it and count the next interval from that real date.

The difference is where the clock starts. A reminder counts from the date you set: clean the air conditioner a week early and next month's alert still fires on the old date, and after two or three snoozes nobody knows when it was really done. A last-done log shows the number you actually want, how many days it has been, and answers the question on the spot.

Split it into three groups. Home: sheets every 1 to 2 weeks, the air-conditioner filter washed every 2 to 4 weeks when it runs daily, a full air-conditioner service every 6 months, the water-filter cartridge every 6 months or per the manual. Car: an oil change at the manual's mileage or every 6 months, whichever comes first, and tyre pressure monthly. Health: a dental check every 6 months, a new toothbrush every 3 months, pet deworming every 3 to 6 months on the vet's advice. What they share is that two weeks late breaks nothing, while six months forgotten costs money.

The method is to log at the moment you finish, not when you happen to remember, because memory goes the moment the tools are put away. If you only remember days later, an approximate backdated entry beats a blank. Mochi DoneStamp works this way: one tap stores the exact time, approximate or exact rhythms run from minutes to years, the next due date restarts from the real finish, and there are Home and Lock Screen widgets. What to check before you choose

  • Whether the item has a true deadline (car tax, an appointment) or just needs to recur
  • How many weeks or months sit between repeats: the longer, the more a log is worth
  • Whether the next interval should shift when you do it early
  • Whether someone else at home sometimes does it, so it is not done twice
  • Whether your tool lets you backdate an entry when you log two days late

The steps that actually work

  1. List everything that recurs, then cross out anything with a real deadline; those stay on the calendar.
  2. Sort the rest into home, car and health and enter the last time you remember, roughly the month is enough.
  3. Give each a rough interval from the numbers above, then adjust for your home: pets, dust, daily driving.
  4. Log the moment you finish, before putting anything away; if a reminder also fired, tap Done, not Snooze.
  5. Once a month, look only at what comes due in the next four weeks and ignore the rest.

FAQ

If I do it early, do I have to fix the reminder every time?

With a calendar reminder, yes, by hand, and that is where most people give up. With a last-done log the next interval restarts from the real date on its own.

I forgot to log it when I finished. Now what?

Enter an approximate backdated entry as soon as you remember, such as early this month. It beats a blank, and you can correct it if a receipt or photo turns up.
