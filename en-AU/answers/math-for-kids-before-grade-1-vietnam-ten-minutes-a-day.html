Math before first grade: a ten-minute daily plan at home

Parents searching for maths for kids are usually worried about one thing: first grade is coming and they are not sure their child is ready. What a child needs is not piles of worksheets, but to understand numbers through pictures and to practise a little every day.

Before first grade, a child should count and recognise numbers to 20, understand splitting and combining within 10, meet addition and subtraction within 10 through pictures, and compare more and fewer. Ten minutes a day with picture questions (counting sticks, ten frames, number lines) works better than one long weekend session.

First-grade maths typically takes children to numbers within 100 and addition and subtraction without regrouping. A child does not need to learn that in advance; what matters is a solid base: accurate counting, recognising numbers, and understanding that adding makes more and taking away makes fewer, within 10. With that base, the first weeks of school are much easier.

Five- and six-year-olds understand numbers through pictures before symbols. Ten frames, counting sticks, dice and number lines let a child see that 7 is 5 and 2, or that 10 take away 3 leaves 7. Once they can see it, they can work it out in their head instead of counting fingers, and written sums stop being mysterious symbols.

Pressure is what makes many children afraid of maths before school even starts. Do not turn practice into a test: if an answer is wrong, try again without criticism or comparison with other children. One right answer praised immediately does more than ten corrections. If the child is tired, stop after ten minutes and pick it up tomorrow.

Beyond adding and subtracting, children meet clocks and money every day. Reading the hour before school and counting coins at the market turn maths into part of daily life, so the clock lesson in first grade already feels familiar.

What to check before you choose

  • Are questions picture-based, so a child who cannot read yet can answer?
  • Can questions be read aloud?
  • Are wrong answers met with buzzers or penalties?
  • Can parents see the weak spots?
  • Are there printable worksheets for paper practice?
  • Any ads or purchase screens a child could tap by mistake?

The steps that actually work

  1. Pick a fixed ten-minute slot each day.
  2. Week one: count and recognise numbers to 10 with pictures.
  3. Week two: split and combine within 10 using a ten frame.
  4. Week three: add and subtract within 10, pictures before symbols.
  5. Weekends: read the hour and count money in everyday life.
  6. Once a week, review the weak spots and practise just those.

FAQ

Should a five-year-old already add and subtract?

Start with pictures within 10, adding to and taking away from counting sticks or a ten frame. There is no need to push written symbols too early.

How long should practice be?

About ten minutes is enough for a preschooler. Every day beats one long weekend session.

Can a child who cannot read yet use the app?

Yes. Lumi Math Planet questions are picture-based and can be read aloud.

Does the app have ads?

No. Lumi Math Planet has no ads, needs no account and plays offline.
