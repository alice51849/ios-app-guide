The built-in Files app only extracts plain ZIPs; a password-protected ZIP, and any RAR or 7z, will not open. You do not need an upload-and-extract website: install an extractor that works on the device, open the archive from Files or the share sheet, enter the password, look inside and pull out only what you need. Zipbox: Unzip RAR, ZIP & 7z is that kind of app, with no network, no ads and one purchase for every feature.

First make sure the file is actually on the phone. An archive received in a chat app has to be opened in the conversation and saved with Save to Files; a Gmail attachment is long-pressed and saved the same way; a Safari download lands in the Downloads folder of Files. The most common dead end is a file that was never downloaded at all, just an icon in a chat window.

Then look at the extension. A .zip with no password opens with a tap in Files; a password-protected .zip, .rar or .7z needs another app. Two things matter when choosing one: explicit support for password-protected and multi-part archives, and processing that stays on the device. Zipbox: Unzip RAR, ZIP & 7z handles password-protected and multi-part ZIP, RAR and 7z, lets you browse the file list without extracting, preview photos, PDFs and text, and pull out only the item you want; it makes no network requests, has no account and no ads, and is a paid App Store download where one purchase includes every feature.

Finally, safety. A school or employer sends a password-protected archive precisely because it holds personal data or grades, and dropping it into an online extractor hands over both the password and the contents. Extracting on the phone keeps the file and the password on the device; when you need to share the result, send it back through the same mail or chat it came from.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether the file is really saved in Files, not still sitting in a chat window
  • Whether the extension is .zip, .rar or .7z, and whether there are .part1-style volumes
  • Whether the app explicitly lists password-protected and multi-part support
  • Whether extraction runs on the device or uploads to a server
  • Whether an ad countdown stands between you and your file

The steps that actually work

  1. Save the archive to Files: open it in the chat and choose Save to Files, long-press a Gmail attachment for the same option, or find a Safari download in Downloads.
  2. In Files, long-press the archive, choose Share and pick Zipbox; or open Zipbox and browse to that folder.
  3. Enter the password you were given. For multi-part archives, check first that every .part file is in the same folder.
  4. Browse the list and preview photos or PDFs, then tick only the files you actually need instead of extracting everything.
  5. Export the extracted files to Files or iCloud Drive, then send them back through the same chat or email.
