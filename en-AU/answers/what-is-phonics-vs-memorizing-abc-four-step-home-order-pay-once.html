Phonics: from memorising letter names to hearing letter sounds

A child can sing the ABC song perfectly yet cannot read cat, which is why many parents start looking into phonics. A letter's name is "A is ay"; phonics is "what sound a makes in cat". They are different things, and the order matters.

Phonics helps children connect letters with the sounds they stand for so they can hear and blend words. At home, go in four steps: first tell each letter's name from its sound, then practise hearing the first sound of a word, then the last sound, and finally the hardest one, the middle sound, followed by reading words from pictures. A short session every day works better than one long one.

Memorising ABC teaches only letter names, but reading words needs letter sounds. So the first step of phonics is hearing both the letter name and the letter sound, knowing that b is called bee and says /b/. Lumi Letters Pro starts with letter names and sounds, then moves to first, last and middle sounds and finally reading from pictures, the order commonly used in English-speaking preschools.

Why do middle sounds come last? First and last sounds are easiest to hear; the vowel in the middle is hardest. A child can hear the shared first sound /k/ in cat, cup and car, then the shared ending in cat and hat, and only later tell the different middle sounds of cat and cut. The app's 14 planets and 12 question types build up step by step, including listening and choosing letters, finding first and last sounds from pictures, matching capitals and lowercase, and filling missing letters.

What to check before you choose

  • Does it teach both letter names and letter sounds, not just the ABC song?
  • Does it build from first to last to middle sounds by difficulty?
  • Is there picture reading that blends sounds into words?
  • Can parents see progress in skills such as phonemic awareness?
  • Is it a one-time purchase with no subscription and no ads?

The steps that actually work

  1. Let your child hear each letter's name and sound and tell them apart.
  2. Practise first sounds with words that share one (cat, cup, car).
  3. Once first sounds are solid, practise hearing shared last sounds.
  4. Practise the hardest, middle sounds last, such as the difference between cat and cut.
  5. Read words from pictures, and write practised letters on paper with a worksheet.

FAQ

How is phonics different from phonetic symbols such as KK?

Phonics reads sounds straight from letters to blend words; phonetic symbols are a separate notation. Young children usually start with phonics.

At what age can phonics start?

It depends on the child. Once they can sing ABC and recognise some letters, you can start slowly with letter sounds and first sounds.

Why can't my child hear the middle sound?

The vowel in the middle is naturally the hardest to hear. Get first and last sounds solid first, and middle sounds become easier.
