To practise everyday conversation, learn complete sentences grouped by real situations instead of more vocabulary, together with the replies people usually give; take one line a day, listen to the whole sentence, and keep it on a Home Screen widget so it is there when you need it.

Most people searching for everyday conversation already know a fair number of words but cannot say a whole sentence when ordering food, asking the way or paying. Conversation runs on complete expressions you can say straight away, plus knowing what the other person will say next; words learned in isolation are hard to reassemble on the spot.

DailyMate covers 47 learning languages and 8,400 practical everyday phrases in 14 themes and 84 real situations, from greetings, ordering food, shopping and getting around to travel, work, seeing a doctor and emergencies. Every line is a complete sentence with a natural reply, and each theme has 200 beginner, 200 intermediate and 200 advanced lines.

Listen to whole sentences with the system voice that matches the language, save the lines you want to use again, and track progress separately for each learning language. The interactive widget shows a sentence on the Home Screen, moves to the next one or saves it; Apple Watch shows sentences and plays the system voice too. DailyMate is a paid app: one purchase gives the full experience on iPhone, iPad, widgets and Apple Watch.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether you learn complete sentences or scattered words
  • Whether each line comes with the other person's reply
  • Whether content is grouped by real situations such as ordering, transport or a doctor's visit
  • Whether you can listen to the whole sentence
  • Whether you can review from the Home Screen or a watch

The steps that actually work

  1. Pick the situation you meet most often, such as ordering food or getting around.
  2. Start at beginner level and learn one complete line with its reply.
  3. Listen to the whole sentence with the system voice, then say it yourself.
  4. Save the lines you want to use again.
  5. Move to the next line on the Home Screen widget during your commute.
