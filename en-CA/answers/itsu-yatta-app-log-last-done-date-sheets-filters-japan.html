Log the date and time with one tap the moment you finish. Recording what you actually did and seeing how many days have passed works better than piling up reminders in advance. Add a rhythm only to things with a real interval, and count the next time from the day you actually finished.

You lose track because you record when you remember, not when you finish. Writing it into a calendar later means the date is already fuzzy. Reminders tell you the day something is due, but if you do it late or early, every later date drifts. So the habit that lasts is a single tap that stamps the time the moment the job is done.

It pays off most for things done every two weeks to every six months: changing sheets and pillowcases, cleaning the shower drain, AC or range-hood filters, water-filter cartridges, watering and repotting plants, replacing toothbrushes and sponges, calling family, backing up phone photos, haircuts and dental checkups. Each is a classic "last month… or the month before?" item.

Then think about rhythms. Put a deadline on everything and you'll get so many alerts you start ignoring them, so add a loose rhythm such as "about every two weeks" or an exact one such as "every 90 days" only where it matters. The key is that the next time is counted from the day you actually finished. Change the sheets three days late and the next change is two weeks from then.

Mochi DoneStamp is built for exactly this. One tap logs a finished task, with Undo, and you can add or edit past dates. It supports rhythms from minutes to years, local reminders with Done and Snooze, Home and Lock Screen widgets, Siri and Shortcuts, and optional notes and photos. The free version starts with one active event and unlimited history; a one-time Lifetime Pro purchase removes the event limit. No account, no ads, no tracking and no subscription.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether one tap records the date and time the moment you finish
  • Whether you can add or fix dates you forgot to log
  • Whether the next due time restarts from the day you actually finished
  • Whether you can choose loose or exact rhythms
  • Whether you can log from Home Screen or Lock Screen widgets
  • Whether it works without an account or ads

The steps that actually work

  1. Write down three things you recently couldn't remember doing (for example sheets, filters, toothbrush).
  2. Add the one that bothers you most as an event.
  3. The next time you finish it, tap once to log it, and add any past date you remember.
  4. Give a rhythm only to things with a fixed interval, and keep alerts to the ones you need.
  5. Put a widget on the Home Screen or Lock Screen so you can log it on the spot.

FAQ

How is it different from a to-do list or reminders?

A to-do list tracks what you still have to do and reminders track the day something is due. Mochi DoneStamp keeps the day you actually did it, shows how many days have passed, and counts the next time from there.

What if I forget to log it?

You can add past dates and edit or delete entries. Enter the last date you remember and the next time is calculated from it.

Can a family log the same chores?

Records are stored on the device and no account is needed. If several people log, agree on one device and widget for it so nobody wonders where the latest date is.
