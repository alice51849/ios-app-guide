How to keep using a to-do list: one three-minute morning routine

The days you skip the list are not the busy days; they are the days with no slot for it. Fix the slot and busy days stop breaking the streak.

Attach three minutes to something you already do every morning, while the coffee brews or as soon as you sit on the train. One minute to tick or move yesterday's leftovers, one to write today's five, one to set a reminder on the first item. Hold that order for two weeks and it becomes a habit.

The way to-do lists fail is nearly always the same. Week one you write whenever something comes to mind, week two you start saying "I'll sort it later", and by week three yesterday's list is still sitting there untouched and opening the app feels bad. The problem is not willpower; it is that writing has no fixed time. A list written "when I think of it" is the first thing dropped on a busy day.

So you bolt three minutes onto an existing daily action: the coffee machine running, the first seat on the train, sitting down in the lecture hall. Minute one is yesterday: tick what got done, move the rest to today or delete it outright. Minute two is today: write up to five items. Minute three is the first item: give it a reminder time, and that alert becomes the second moment of the day the list gets opened.

For two weeks, nothing happens to the list outside those three minutes: no decorating, no reorganising. The same three minutes stacking up removes the "what should I do today" deliberation, and opening the list becomes part of the morning rather than a separate chore. Mochi is light enough for that window: write, tap the little circle to finish, set a reminder, and see today's list on a Home Screen widget or Apple Watch. Free to start, no ads.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether you can open the app and have the first item typed within three taps
  • How easy it is to move yesterday's unfinished items to today
  • Whether reminders can be set per item
  • Whether today's list shows on a Home Screen widget or on your wrist
  • Whether ads appear or an account is demanded first

The steps that actually work

  1. Pick one thing you already do every morning: coffee, train, brushing teeth. The moment right after it is your three-minute slot.
  2. Minute one: open yesterday, tick what is done, move or delete what is not. No deliberating.
  3. Minute two: write today's items, five at most. When five are in, the rest go to a later list.
  4. Minute three: set a reminder on the first item for a time you can genuinely start, say 10 am.
  5. Same anchor, same order, for two weeks. Decorating and reorganising wait for one weekend session.

FAQ

What about mornings when I do not even have three minutes?

Do one. Move yesterday's leftovers now and write today's five at lunch instead. What matters is that the app was opened, not that the routine was complete.

Evenings work better for me. Is that fine?

Yes, as long as the evening slot is also attached to an action, such as right after brushing your teeth. What gets fixed is the anchor action, not the clock time.
