Keep one notebook per course, handwrite on top of the lecturer's PDF slides, record audio on the page you're writing, and turn definitions into flashcards right away. Before the exam you search your handwriting, play the explanation attached to that exact page and review cards on a schedule instead of rereading everything.

The main mistake is mixing every course in one place. Create a notebook per course and pick paper that suits it: grid for math, lines for humanities, staves for music theory. If the lecturer shares slides, import the PDF and write right on top: highlights, underlines and handwritten notes stay next to the source instead of in a separate file.

Second, sound. You can't write down a fast lecturer word for word, so record audio on the page you're currently writing. The recording is attached to that page and plays from it, so a month later you open the topic itself, not "Recording 37". Handwriting recognition and search across titles, text, handwriting and attachments run on the device and work offline, even in a lecture hall with poor signal.

Third, review. Notes alone don't prepare you for an exam. Move definitions, formulas and dates into study-mode flashcards with cloze deletions and FSRS spaced repetition, which shows a card when you're about to forget it. Two or three weeks before exams you review 15 to 20 minutes a day instead of cramming the night before.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether you can handwrite directly on the lecturer's PDF
  • Whether audio recordings attach to a specific page
  • Whether handwriting is searchable, and whether search works offline
  • Whether notes can become spaced-repetition flashcards
  • Whether pressure, tilt and palm rejection are supported
  • Whether it needs an account or shows ads

The steps that actually work

  1. Create a notebook per course with suitable paper.
  2. Import the lecturer's slides as PDF and annotate on top.
  3. In class, record audio on the page you're writing.
  4. After class, move definitions and formulas into study-mode flashcards.
  5. Two to three weeks before exams, review the cards on schedule for 15 to 20 minutes a day.

FAQ

Does it work offline?

Yes. Core features including writing, handwriting recognition and search run on the device, so weak signal in a lecture hall doesn't matter.

How do I find the right spot in a lecture recording?

The recording is attached to the page where you started it and plays from that page, so opening the topic gives you its explanation.

Is the free mode enough for a semester?

Free mode includes 1 active notebook of up to 5 pages and 2 styles, enough to try it. Notes for several courses need the additional unlock.
