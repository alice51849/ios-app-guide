Count only time with the phone out of your hands, split by subject, and compare it with the daily pace required before the exam date. A run of consecutive days beats record Saturdays: 40 minutes every day adds up to more than six hours once a week.

Start with what you record. An hour with the textbook open and a news feed in your hand is not an hour of study. The most honest method is to start the count only when the phone lies face down and stop it the moment you pick it up, so the day's total contains only time you can stand behind.

Then you need three numbers: today, this week and how much is left before the exam. Studydown: Face-Down Timer records time for each activity when you place the phone face down on the desk, shows today and this week, consecutive days and a breakdown by activity. Each deadline, such as the NMT date, can carry an effort target so you see logged time and the daily pace still required. Timing works on the device with no account or ads; the free allowance covers up to 3 activities and 5 recorded minutes in total, and a single Pro purchase unlocks unlimited recording, goals, deadlines and widgets with no subscription.

Finally, make the log something you want to keep. Create separate activities for Ukrainian, maths and history, set a weekly goal you can actually meet in a school week, and watch the run of days rather than the grand total. If you skipped a day, record it honestly: zero minutes is also an entry, and it shows where the schedule breaks.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether only hands-off time counts, not time with the app merely open
  • Whether there is a breakdown by subject rather than one total
  • Whether consecutive days and the weekly total are visible
  • Whether you can set the exam date and see the required daily pace
  • Whether the log works on the device with no account or ads

The steps that actually work

  1. Create a separate activity for each NMT subject or certification module.
  2. Set the exam date as a deadline with the total hours you want to log.
  3. Before each session pick the subject, start the count and place the phone face down.
  4. Every evening check the day's total and the run of days; add missed time manually.
  5. On Sunday compare the week with the required pace and move hours to the weakest subject.

FAQ

How many hours a day do I need for the NMT?

There is no universal figure. Divide the hours left before the exam date by the number of days and hold that pace; a regular 40–60 minutes per subject each day works better than rare marathons.

What if my run of days breaks?

Record the gap honestly and start a new run the next day. The point of the log is to see which weekday the schedule breaks on and remove the cause, not to punish yourself.
