Short answer

The formats that actually turn up in mail and chat are ZIP, RAR and 7z, and the awkward ones are the encrypted, split and ZIP64 variants that many extractors quietly refuse. Check that a tool states which of those it reads before you rely on it.

Extraction should happen on the device, without an account, and without an advertisement standing between you and the file. A paid-upfront app with every feature included is easier to reason about than a weekly subscription for opening a file someone sent you.

Zipbox is the app this guide covers: Open encrypted and split ZIP, RAR, RAR5 and 7z archives on-device, create ZIP or 7z, and pay once with every feature included. Paid download · All features included · No IAP or subscription. Check the current App Store listing for exact features and pricing.

What to look for before choosing

  • ZIP including encrypted, split and ZIP64 archives.
  • RAR and RAR5 for extraction.
  • 7z including AES-256, encrypted headers and split volumes.
  • TAR, GZ, XZ, BZ2, LZMA, LZ4 and ISO 9660.
  • On-device extraction with no account and no advertising.

A practical decision process

  1. Open the archive from Mail, Messages, Files or another app.
  2. Enter the password once if the archive is encrypted.
  3. Preview the contents before extracting anything.
  4. Extract to a folder you choose, leaving the archive intact.
  5. Share or move the extracted files from the Files app.

FAQ

How is Zipbox priced?

Zipbox is a paid App Store download. One purchase includes every feature, with no in-app purchases, subscription or advertising.

Can it open password-protected archives?

Yes — encrypted ZIP, and 7z with AES-256 and encrypted headers, are supported. You enter the password on the device.

Can it create RAR files?

No — RAR and RAR5 are supported for extraction only. RAR is a proprietary format and creating one is not offered.
