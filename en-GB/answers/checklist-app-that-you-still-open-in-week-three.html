Checklists: how to build one that survives week three

Almost everyone has abandoned a checklist app. The app is rarely the problem — the list grew faster than it was finished, and it lived somewhere you stopped looking.

Keep a today list of four items and a second list for the rest of the week, put today where you see it without opening an app, and make every tick visible. Those rules fix most abandoned checklists, and they fit inside Mochi's free version, which holds 2 lists with up to 4 tasks each.

A list that piles up to 60 items stops being a tool and turns into a guilt reminder. Four items is a number most people can actually finish on a weekday after work or class. Everything else goes on a second list for the rest of the week, which you look at in the evening and again on Sunday.

The second cause is having three places: phone notes, a paper list on the fridge, and an app. When the same errand lives in three places, none of them is trustworthy, so you stop checking all of them. Pick one place for a week and accept that it will be incomplete for the first few days while everything moves over.

The third cause is visibility. The checklist you forget is the one hidden two taps deep inside an app. A Home Screen widget puts today's items where you already look dozens of times a day, and checking the list from Apple Watch means you can see the next errand while your hands are full in a grocery aisle. Mochi supports both: widgets for the Home Screen and your list on Apple Watch, plus reminders that keep you on track.

Fourth is the feeling of finishing. Ticking has to return something right away. In Mochi you tap the little circle when a task is done, and the list visibly gets shorter. It sounds cosmetic; it is what gets the app opened again tomorrow, and a checklist only works if it is opened every day.

It helps to know the boundary before you download. The free version of Mochi includes 2 lists with up to 4 tasks each, 5 themes and 1 emoji set — exactly enough to run the today-plus-this-week method below. If you later want separate lists for groceries, school, chores and routines, Premium is an in-app purchase that unlocks unlimited lists and tasks, all 101 themes and all 11 emoji sets. Run the method for one week on the free version first; by Sunday you will know whether you need more lists.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether you can keep today's list short and a second list for later
  • Whether a Home Screen widget shows today's tasks without opening the app
  • Whether you can check the list from Apple Watch
  • Whether completing a task gives immediate, visible feedback
  • How many taps adding a task costs
  • What the free version holds before you pay, and whether the upgrade is a subscription

The steps that actually work

  1. Spend ten minutes writing everything you are carrying in your head into one place, unfiltered.
  2. Choose the four tasks that must happen today and put them on a list called Today. Everything else goes on a list called This week.
  3. Add the Home Screen widget so Today is visible every time you unlock your phone.
  4. For one week, nothing new enters Today once it holds four tasks; new things go to This week.
  5. Each evening, tick what is done and move up to four tasks from This week into tomorrow's Today.
  6. On Sunday, delete anything that has sat untouched on This week for three weeks instead of postponing it again.

FAQ

Isn't four tasks too few?

Deliberately. A finished list of four is worth more than a list of twenty with three crossed off. On a lighter day you can pull the next task from This week.

Where do big tasks go?

On This week, broken into steps. Only the step that fits in one day moves into Today.

Can I use the same app for groceries and chores?

Yes. Mochi is built for tasks, errands, groceries, chores and routines. On the free version that means choosing which 2 lists matter most; Premium removes the list and task limits.

Does Mochi have a subscription?

No. Mochi is free to start; Premium is a one-time in-app purchase that unlocks unlimited lists and tasks, all 101 themes and all 11 emoji sets.
