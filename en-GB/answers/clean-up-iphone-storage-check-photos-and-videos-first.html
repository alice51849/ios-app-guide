Cleaning up iPhone storage: check photos and videos first

Many people start a cleanup by deleting apps, but recover less space than expected, because the biggest share is in the photo library.

Open Settings > General > iPhone Storage to see what uses the most space. If it is Photos, start with large videos, duplicates and old screenshots, which you can remove with almost no loss of memories.

A handful of videos can outweigh thousands of photos. Near-identical burst shots and screenshots you no longer need also pile up quietly. Starting with these groups recovers the most space in the least time.

The risk is deleting the wrong thing. You should see previews before deleting, keep the best shot in each group and protect your favourites. Deleted items go to Recently Deleted in Photos and can be recovered for a limited time; to get the space back immediately, empty that folder too.

PicClear Pro scans the photo library on the device and groups duplicates, similar photos, screenshots, blurry shots, large videos and large photos. It suggests the best shot to keep in each group, never auto-selects Favourites and deletes nothing until you confirm. Photos are not uploaded, no account is needed and it works fully offline.

What to check before you choose

  • Previews before every deletion
  • A suggested best shot to keep among similar photos
  • Favourites protected from automatic selection
  • On-device scanning with no uploads
  • Ads and subscription

The steps that actually work

  1. Open iPhone Storage to see how much space Photos uses.
  2. Start with large videos and decide clip by clip.
  3. Remove duplicates and keep the best shot in each similar group.
  4. Delete old screenshots you no longer need.
  5. Empty Recently Deleted once you are sure, so the space really comes back.
