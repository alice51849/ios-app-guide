Cycle length is the number of days from the first day of one period to the first day of the next. Start from the first day of bleeding, not the last. After three or four cycles, the shortest and longest lengths — your range — say more than any single number.

The most common mistake is counting from the day a period ends. A period can last three days one month and seven the next, so counting from the end makes cycle length jump around with it. The correct count is the days between two first days: a period that starts on 3 October and again on 31 October is a 28-day cycle.

One cycle on its own says little. Neighbouring cycles commonly differ by two or three days, and stress, sleep and travel all move them. What is useful is the shortest and longest after three or four consecutive cycles — your own range. Knowing which few days the next period is likely to fall on is more practical than a single fixed date.

If cycles become much shorter or longer than usual, stop for several months, or pain and flow change noticeably, that is a reason to see a clinician, not something an app can judge. What to check before you choose

  • Whether logging starts from the first day of bleeding, and whether past days can be added later
  • Whether estimates use your own records or assume a fixed 28 days
  • Whether you can see each cycle's length, not just an average
  • Whether data stays on the phone or goes to a cloud account
  • Whether cramps and flow can be logged too, for a doctor's visit

The steps that actually work

  1. Log the first day of bleeding on the day it happens, not afterwards.
  2. Log three or four consecutive cycles, counting only first day to next first day.
  3. Write down the shortest and longest lengths — that is your current range.
  4. Use the range to see which days the next period is likely to start, and prepare.
  5. If the range suddenly widens or periods stop for months, take your records to a clinician.

FAQ

Which day does a cycle start on?

The first day of bleeding. It runs until the day before the next period starts; the day bleeding ends is not the start.

My cycle was three days different from last time. Is that normal?

A few days of difference between cycles is common. More telling than one difference is whether the range over several cycles suddenly widens, or keeps drifting from what is usual for you.
