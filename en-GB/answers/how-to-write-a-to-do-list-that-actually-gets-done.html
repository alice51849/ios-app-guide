How to write a to-do list that gets done

The same errands can become a list you finish or a list you only stare at, depending on how it is written.

A to-do list gets done when every item starts with a verb, is small enough to start right away, and today's list holds five items at most. Everything else goes on a separate list.

Writing just a noun like "holiday" or "taxes" leaves you unsure where to start, so it gets postponed. Specific actions that start with a verb — "compare three hotels", "find last year's tax form" — get your hands moving.

Break big jobs down until the first step takes 5 to 15 minutes. Items that sit on a list for weeks are almost always too big.

Keep today's list to five and send the rest to a someday list. Mochi is a cute checklist app: tap the little circle and it is done. Home and Lock Screen widgets and an Apple Watch app keep the list in view without opening it. No account, no ads. The free version includes 2 lists with up to 4 tasks each; Premium, an in-app purchase, unlocks unlimited lists and tasks.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether items can be added quickly
  • Whether a widget shows today's list
  • Whether completing takes one tap
  • Whether no sign-up is needed
  • Whether there are no ads

The steps that actually work

  1. Write down every errand in your head.
  2. Rewrite noun-only items as actions starting with a verb.
  3. Split anything that would take more than 15 minutes.
  4. Keep only the five you will do today on today's list.
  5. Tick each as soon as it is done and review the rest at night.

An item stays on my list every day.

It is probably too big. Split it into an action you can do in the first 15 minutes.
