An iPhone's battery capacity (maximum capacity) is how much charge the battery can hold now compared with when it was new, shown in Settings › Battery. Apple uses 80% as a service benchmark, but a replacement decision should look at how much it drops per month, together with cycle count and the symptoms you actually see, such as fast drain or heat.

Settings only shows today's maximum capacity. A phone that has sat at 86% for half a year and one that fell from 92% to 86% in three months are very different, even though today's number is the same. Writing the number down every so often tells you more than one look.

A third-party iPhone app cannot read maximum capacity or cycle count by itself. iOS lets apps read only battery level, charge state, Low Power Mode, whole-device thermal state and time, so be wary of an app that claims to read capacity automatically. The capacity value has to come from what you enter from Settings or from the system's Analytics file.

The steps that actually work

  1. Open Settings › Battery and note today's maximum capacity.
  2. Enter it in BattAI to see the score and the main factor behind it.
  3. Enter it again every two to four weeks; once readings are 30 days apart you see the monthly drop.
  4. Turn on the full charge alert at 80% or 90% if that suits how you use the phone.
  5. If the number nears 80% or the battery drains unusually fast, get it checked by Apple or a service provider.

FAQ

At what battery capacity should I replace an iPhone battery?

Apple uses 80% as a service benchmark. If capacity is still above that but the battery drains unusually fast, or it dropped quickly within a few months, it is still worth getting checked. BattAI's score is calculated, not an Apple diagnosis.

Does BattAI read battery capacity automatically?

No. iOS does not let apps read maximum capacity. You enter the value from Settings › Battery or import it from the system's Analytics file.
