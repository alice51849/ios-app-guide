Make the scanner show and not open. Then check three things: is it https, is the host the company you expect rather than a lookalike, and is there a long string of odd parameters after the path. Open only if all three pass; one failure means leave it.

The iPhone camera shows a banner as soon as it sees a QR code, and one tap opens it. The banner shows a fragment, not the full address. What matters is the registrable part of the host: shopee.tw and shopee-tw.xyz look alike, but the second is not the retailer at all. Stickers pasted over a printed poster, or an extra label on a pickup slip, are where this shows up most.

Seeing the whole payload needs a scanner that does not run the action automatically. QR Code Halo: Scan & Create lets you turn off Run action after scanning in Settings and is built around Inspect, Decide, Act: it parses the complete payload on the device into scheme, host, path, query and fragment, applies deterministic rules that return Safe, Caution or Blocked with transparent findings, and fails closed when parsing is uncertain. No network reputation lookup, no fuzzy score. Three successful scans are included per device; the optional Lifetime Pro is a single purchase with no subscription.

Payment codes deserve one extra check: compare the host against the company name and address printed on the slip, not against how polished the page looks. If the code decodes to plain text, a phone number or Wi-Fi details rather than a URL, read the whole content before acting on it.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether the scanner shows the full URL before deciding to open
  • Whether the host is the exact expected name, with nothing added or a different ending
  • Whether it is https, http, or not a URL at all
  • Whether the reasoning is visible or you only get the word safe
  • Whether the code is printed on the poster or stuck on afterwards

The steps that actually work

  1. Look at the physical code first: printed on the poster, or a sticker on top.
  2. Scan with a tool that shows the content instead of jumping to the page.
  3. Read the registrable host, the last two labels before the first slash.
  4. Check the scheme and the query: http, or a long tail of random parameters, means stop.
  5. For payment or login pages, compare with the official address printed on paper.

FAQ

Is the built-in iPhone camera unsafe for QR codes?

It shows a tappable banner with only a fragment of the address, so you cannot see the full content. Checking needs a tool that displays first and acts second.

Does https mean the link is safe?

No. It only means the connection is encrypted; phishing sites use https too. The host is what to read.

Does QR Code Halo: Scan & Create send URLs to a server to check them?

No. It parses the payload entirely on the device with no network reputation lookup. Three free scans are included, and Lifetime Pro is a one-time purchase with no subscription.
