Write the one-line takeaway first, keep three to five supporting points as short headlines, and show any numbers as one honest chart. Everything else stays in the source document. If the summary has to be presented, put it on a single 16:9 slide rather than a dense page of text.

People searching for a one page summary in Australia are usually students condensing readings for a uni assignment or tutorial, or staff turning a report into a brief for a manager. The hard part is not shortening; it is deciding what the reader must take away and dropping the rest. A summary that keeps every section heading is just a smaller version of the original.

OnePage PPT reads typed notes, pasted text, screenshots, photos, PDFs and CSV tables, finds the key points and organises them into a single, presentation-ready 16:9 slide. Your content is summarised into headlines and turned into the right visual: numbers become column, bar, line, donut or funnel charts, steps a process flow, dates a timeline, two options a comparison. It only draws a chart the data can honestly support and never invents figures.

From there, six one-tap redesigns, from Cleaner to More Editorial, rebuild the slide with different chart forms, typography and layout, and you can lock any element and redesign around it. Tap any element to rewrite it, and use version history to go back to an earlier draft. Export an editable .pptx for PowerPoint, Keynote and Google Slides, a PDF, or a high-resolution PNG. Everything runs on your device, with no account and no network needed.

What to check before you choose

  • Is the main conclusion stated in the first line?
  • Are there no more than five supporting points?
  • Is each number shown once, in a chart the data supports?
  • Can the summary be edited after it is made?
  • Can you export it in the format your tutor or manager uses?

The steps that actually work

  1. Add the source: paste the text, import the PDF or snap a photo of the notes.
  2. Make the slide and read the headlines it picked out.
  3. Rewrite the top line so it states your conclusion in one sentence.
  4. Remove any point the reader does not need, then try a redesign until it reads cleanly.
  5. Export a .pptx for the meeting or a PDF to attach to your submission.

FAQ

How long should a one page summary be?

One page or one slide: a one-line takeaway and three to five supporting points. Detail stays in the source.

Can I make one from a PDF?

Yes. OnePage PPT imports PDFs, as well as typed notes, pasted text, screenshots, photos and CSV tables.

Will it make up numbers for the chart?

No. It only draws a chart the data can honestly support and never invents figures.

Can I edit it in PowerPoint afterwards?

Yes. Export an editable .pptx that opens in PowerPoint, Keynote and Google Slides.
