Airport arrival sign: the right text, the right size, no oversharing

In an arrivals hall, the person coming out scans dozens of signs for two or three seconds. What you write matters as much as how big it is.

Write what the person will recognize instantly: their surname in capitals, or a first name plus initial (“MARIE D.”), or a code word agreed in advance. Avoid pairing the full name with the hotel or company. Aim for letters at least 8–10 cm tall, strong contrast (yellow or white on black) and no more than two lines.

Surname only is the professional drivers' convention: short, readable from afar, and easy to spot even when tired. For a relative or a guest who has never met you, first name plus initial is enough. A full name with a hotel or company name, however, gives any stranger enough to introduce themselves in your place, which is why many agencies suggest a code word or a shortened form.

Readability depends mainly on letter height and contrast. From about ten metres, letters a few centimetres tall are unreadable; you need bold characters filling the full width. Static text reads faster than scrolling text; scrolling only helps when the name is too long to fit large. An unusual colour also helps you stand out from rows of white signs.

If you are meeting several travellers or the flight is delayed, plan to alternate: the name, then a practical note such as “EXIT B” or “TAXI”. Hold the sign at chest height facing the exit doors and stay at the exact spot you shared by message.

What to check before you choose

  • Letters large enough to read from several metres
  • Static display for a short name, scrolling for longer text
  • A contrasting text colour that differs from other signs
  • Rotating the text to use the full height of the screen
  • Moving through several messages without leaving full screen

The steps that actually work

  1. Agree on the text in advance: surname, first name plus initial, or a code word.
  2. Write it in capitals, one line if possible, with no hotel or company name.
  3. Pick a bright colour on a dark background and set the largest readable size.
  4. Check readability by placing the screen ten steps away and looking yourself.
  5. Stand facing the doors with the sign at chest height and message your exact position.

FAQ

Should I write the full name on the sign?

It is not necessary and often discouraged. Surname only or first name plus initial is enough to be recognized, and an agreed code word stops a stranger from stepping in as the traveller.

Is a phone screen big enough for a pickup sign?

For a short name, yes, as long as the letters fill the screen: rotating the text to use the full height and keeping it static makes a five-to-eight-letter name readable from a distance. For a long name, scrolling helps.

Which colour stands out best in an arrivals hall?

Light, saturated text (yellow, white, bright green) on black gives the best contrast. Choosing a colour other signs are not using also helps you stand out.
