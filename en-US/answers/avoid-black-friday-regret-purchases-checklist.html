Avoid regret purchases by building a fixed list with reference prices by early November, shopping only that list on the day, and checking every item for fit, compatibility, returns and work-hour cost before you pay.

A regret purchase is rarely the big item you wanted for months. It is the things next to it: the second charger, the blender that ends up in a cupboard, the sweater in a size that runs differently with that brand. On Black Friday, November 27 this year, with Cyber Monday on November 30, countdown timers and “only 3 left” work on exactly these side purchases. So avoiding them starts before the basket.

The second source of regret is the product itself. Check that the model is really the one you compared — sale stock is sometimes an older generation or a cut-down variant with a similar name. Check dimensions, ports, the size chart and the seller's return conditions, especially for marketplace offers from third-party sellers.

The last question is the most honest one: how many hours of work does it cost? HoursTag Lite converts prices into work time from your take-home value per work hour and lets you tag each purchase as Need, Want or Impulse. With “What will a goal cost?” you also see the work behind a bigger plan that an impulse buy would push back.

What to check before you choose

  • Was the item on your list before November?
  • Is it exactly the model you compared, not a variant?
  • Do dimensions, size and ports match what you already own?
  • What are this seller's return conditions?
  • How many work hours does the final price, shipping included, cost?

The steps that actually work

  1. By early November, make a list and note today's price for each item as your reference.
  2. Set a cap in work hours for the whole sale period.
  3. On the day, open only items from the list; new ideas go onto a waiting list.
  4. Before paying, check the model number, dimensions, size chart and return conditions.
  5. Look at the waiting list again after 48 hours and only then decide.

FAQ

How do I spot a fake deal?

Compare with the price you noted yourself weeks earlier. A high crossed-out price says nothing about what the item actually sold for recently.

Black Friday or Cyber Monday — when should I buy?

If an item from your list is below your reference price on Black Friday, buy it then. Waiting for an even better price often ends with buying something on both days.
