Short answer

Follow the employer's file instructions first. UConn notes that applicant tracking systems vary and do not automatically reject candidates; its guidance favors simple, consistent formatting, common file types unless the employer specifies otherwise, and relevant terms from the posting where they accurately describe your qualifications. What to look for before choosing

  • Follow the employer's requested file type before any general rule
  • Use familiar section headings and a simple, consistent reading order
  • Include relevant terms only when they truthfully describe your experience
  • Reopen the final export and verify selectable text, headings and section order
  • Treat any ATS score as an editing estimate, never a guaranteed outcome

A practical decision process

  1. Define the job you need done most often.
  2. Test the app with real content or a realistic scenario.
  3. Check privacy labels and account requirements.
  4. Confirm export and backup options.
  5. Choose the pricing model you are comfortable maintaining.

FAQ

What makes a resume ATS-friendly?

A simple reading order, familiar headings, truthful job-relevant wording and the file type the employer requests can reduce avoidable formatting risk. Employer systems still vary, so no format is guaranteed.
