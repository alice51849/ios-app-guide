With several Claude accounts, put each account's 5-hour limit, weekly limit and exact reset time side by side and sort by 5-hour reset; the account to continue on comes up first, without signing in to each one.

Many people searching for Claude usage run more than one account: a work account from the company, a personal one they pay for, or a Pro and a Max side by side. Each account counts down its 5-hour session limit and weekly limit separately, so the account with more left is not always the one that lasts. An account near its weekly cap can stop you halfway even with a full 5-hour window, which is why comparing accounts means reading both limits and both reset times.

UsageNow puts every account on one screen: work and personal, Pro and Max, side by side, sorted by 5-hour reset, weekly reset, highest or lowest usage, name or service, or dragged into your own order. On Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets you can flip through each account directly, and a tap on the widget refreshes it when you want the latest numbers.

The moment to switch can come from an alert instead of memory. Set the percentage you care about, like 90 or 98, for the 5-hour limit, the weekly limit or all limits; each alert fires once per limit and cycle. Turn on the reset alert and you know when the account that ran out is back. Sign-in happens on each service's own login page and stays in the device's Keychain; there is no UsageNow server and no account to create.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether each account's 5-hour and weekly limits are shown separately
  • Whether accounts can be sorted by 5-hour or weekly reset time
  • Whether you can flip through accounts right on the widget
  • Whether work and personal accounts sit side by side without signing out
  • Whether sign-in stays on the device with no separate server

The steps that actually work

  1. Sign in to each account once on the service's official login page.
  2. Sort accounts by 5-hour reset, or drag them into the order you use.
  3. Add a widget to the Home Screen or Lock Screen and flip through accounts on it.
  4. Set a percentage alert such as 90 or 98 for each account.
  5. Turn on the reset alert and switch back as soon as a limit returns.
