ChatGPT Codex usage is counted in two windows: a 5-hour window and a weekly window. Before a long run, check both; if one is nearly empty, wait for the reset or split the task into smaller steps.

People searching for the Codex limit have often just been stopped mid-task. ChatGPT Codex counts usage in a 5-hour window and a weekly window. The 5-hour window frees up within hours; the weekly one does not. A long agent run that leans hard on both can cost half the week on a Tuesday.

The habit that matters is checking both numbers before each big run, not after. UsageNow shows the ChatGPT Codex 5-hour and weekly windows on Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets and as Apple Watch complications, with a live countdown to each reset and the exact time in your local format. Tap the widget to load the latest numbers.

With several accounts, such as work and personal, sort by 5-hour reset or weekly reset in UsageNow and you see right away which account still has room for the next run. Your own alerts at a percentage like 90 or 98 arrive once per limit and cycle, and a reset alert tells you when capacity is back.

What to check before you choose

  • Are the Codex 5-hour and weekly windows shown separately?
  • Do you see the exact time of the next reset, not just a bar?
  • Can you set an alert at your own percentage?
  • Can you sort several accounts by reset?
  • Does the sign-in stay in your device's Keychain?

The steps that actually work

  1. Sign in on ChatGPT's official login page.
  2. Put a widget with both Codex windows on your Home Screen.
  3. Before a long run, check what is left in the 5-hour and weekly windows.
  4. If one is nearly used up, split the task or wait for the reset.
  5. Create alerts at 90 and turn on a reset alert.

FAQ

How many windows does the Codex limit have?

Two: a 5-hour window and a weekly window. They are counted and reset separately.

What if the weekly window is almost empty?

Move big runs to after the reset, split tasks into smaller steps, or switch to another account that still has room.

Do I give UsageNow my password?

No. You sign in on the service's own login page, the sign-in stays in your device's Keychain, and UsageNow talks only to the service itself.
