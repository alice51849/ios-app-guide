Keep two separate lists, study and chores. Plan the whole week once, 15 minutes on Sunday night, with at most three items per list per day, each written as a subject plus pages, or a chore plus the time you will really do it.

Most students open a planner with good intentions, and the first page ends up holding "biology chapter 4", "laundry", "gift for Mint" and "lose weight" side by side. Nothing says where to start, so decorating the page takes over. The fix is two clearly separate lists, one for study and one for chores, with everything else parked in a later list.

Second, plan once for the whole week rather than every morning. On Sunday night, check the timetable for tests and deadlines, then spread the reading across Monday to Friday, no more than three items a day, each measurable: "maths, exercise 2.3, questions 1–10", not "do maths". Chores work the same way: "laundry, Saturday morning", "bathroom, Wednesday after school", with the real time attached.

Last, there has to be a visible tick every day, because a planner without a tick box becomes an ordinary notebook within a week. Mochi fits this exactly: a study list and a chores list kept apart, tasks with reminders, a little circle to tap when done, a cute theme for each list, and a Home Screen widget that shows today without opening the app. Free to start, no ads.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether lists can be kept separate instead of piled on one page
  • Whether reminders can be set per item, say 4 pm after school
  • Whether a Home Screen widget shows today's items
  • How many taps adding one item takes; past three, it stops being written down
  • Whether there are ads or a required account

The steps that actually work

  1. Create two lists named Study and Chores, with a different theme each so they are told apart at a glance.
  2. Sunday night, 15 minutes: open the timetable and deadlines, write Monday to Friday's reading, at most three items a day.
  3. Make every item measurable: "physics chapter 3, pages 40–52" or "English, 20 vocabulary words", never "read physics".
  4. Add chores with the real day and time, "laundry, Saturday 9 am", and set a reminder 15 minutes before.
  5. Every evening tick what is done and move anything unfinished to the next day immediately; nothing stays overdue for more than one day.

FAQ

Is three a day too few near exams?

Go to four or five in exam weeks, but each item still has to be measurable and finish within an hour. "Read all of biology" will never get ticked.

What if a week of tutoring leaves no time for chores?

Cut the chores list to the two that truly matter that week, such as laundry and clearing the desk, and move the rest to next week during Sunday planning. No guilt required.

Is Mochi a monthly subscription?

No. It is free to start with no ads, and Premium is a single in-app purchase rather than a monthly charge.
