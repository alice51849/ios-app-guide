Cute to do list: how to pick a pretty one that stays practical

Many people look for a cute or aesthetic to do list because the look changes how much they want to open it. The trick is picking one where pretty never gets in the way.

Pick a cute list where adding and finishing tasks is quick, with themes to switch when you get bored and a nice Home Screen widget: the look should invite you in, not create work.

A pleasant look is not frivolous: if opening the list feels good, you open it more often, and a list you open more often is a list that gets done. The risk appears when the app asks you to decorate every item. If building the list takes longer than doing the task, cuteness becomes an obstacle.

So it helps to separate two things: the look you choose once (theme, emoji, widget) and the gesture you repeat every day (adding and finishing). The first can be as detailed as you like; the second has to be one tap. Switching themes now and then keeps things fresh without touching the routine.

Mochi follows that idea: you add tasks, tap the little circle when you finish and choose from 101 themes (5 free, 96 with Premium), with Home Screen widgets and checking your list from Apple Watch. Use it for school, work, errands, groceries, habits, routines and daily goals.

What to check before you choose

  • How many taps it takes to add and finish a task
  • How many themes exist and how many are in the free version
  • Whether the Home Screen widget matches the app's look
  • Whether emoji or small details let you make the list your own
  • Ads, and what needs an in-app purchase

The steps that actually work

  1. Pick a theme you enjoy seeing every day and leave it alone for a week.
  2. Create a short list for today, with up to four tasks.
  3. Put the widget on your Home Screen so the list shows without opening the app.
  4. Tick each task the moment you finish it, with one tap.
  5. After a week, switch themes if you want a fresh look, without changing the routine.
