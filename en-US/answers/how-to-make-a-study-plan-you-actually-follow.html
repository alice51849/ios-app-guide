Work back from the exam date to spread subjects, cap each day at three tasks, tick them every evening, and catch up at the weekend rather than cramming tomorrow. That alone makes a study plan far easier to keep.

The most common failure is filling every day. One missed slot shifts everything and you stop looking at the plan at all. Keep each day to three tasks and leave the weekend free for adjustment.

The second is vague wording like "do English". Write tasks with a clear finish — "50 vocabulary words", "one past paper", "summary notes for chapter 3" — so every tick feels like progress.

The third is days when you never look at the plan. Mochi is a cute checklist app with Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets, reminders and an Apple Watch app, so today's list is naturally in view. Tap the little circle and it is done. The free version includes 2 lists with up to 4 tasks each; Premium, an in-app purchase, unlocks unlimited lists and tasks.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether today's list shows in a widget
  • Whether you can set a reminder for when study starts
  • Whether ticking takes one tap
  • Whether you need to sign up
  • Whether ads break your focus

The steps that actually work

  1. Write down the exam dates and count the days left.
  2. Spread subjects backwards across the days, heaviest first.
  3. Split each day into three tasks with a clear finish.
  4. Set a reminder for the time of the first task.
  5. Tick in the evening and move whatever is left to the weekend.

FAQ

How many hours a day should I study?

Three tasks you can keep every day beat eight hours followed by two days off. Consistency matters more than volume.

Does Mochi create a study schedule automatically?

No. Mochi is a simple checklist that keeps the tasks you write in view so you can tick them off.
