Open a separate ledger the day you take a job and record only that job's income and expenses in it. Tag every expense as materials, subcontractor or travel. At the end, income minus expenses is the real profit, and that figure corrects your next quote.

A six-figure invoice feels good until you subtract the person you paid to share the work, the printing and licensed assets, and the trips to the client's office and the site. What remains can be little more than half. When living expenses and client work sit in the same ledger, you are looking at money received, not money earned, and quotes have to be built on money earned.

The method is two moves: one ledger per job, and three fixed tags. Materials are costs that end with the job, such as printing, assets, licences and consumables. Subcontractor is anything you paid someone else to do. Travel is client visits, site checks and shipping. Keep the tag list at three, because a long list is the reason nobody tags consistently. When the job closes, read the net first, then check what share of income each tag took. On the next similar job, add those three shares back in before you add the margin you want.

MoneyTag is built for exactly this. Every project is its own ledger, and income minus expenses is that project's net. Any entry can carry a tag, and the Tags screen totals the same tag across all projects, so the cost type that keeps eating your margin is visible at once. Overseas clients paying in another currency are fine: each project keeps one base currency, and entries are converted with live or manual rates.   • Whether expenses can be tagged, and the same tag totalled across jobs
  • Whether a foreign-currency payment converts into one base currency per project
  • Whether a closed job can be exported as CSV or PDF for your records or your accountant
  • Whether it is a one-time purchase or a subscription, and whether the data stays on your device

The steps that actually work

  1. Open a new ledger the day you accept the job, named client plus job, and write the quoted amount in the first note so there is something to compare against later.
  2. Record income only when it actually lands, with the date; deposit and final payment as separate entries. Never record the quote as income.
  3. Record every expense the same day and tag it materials, subcontractor or travel. Do not invent a fourth tag.
  4. On the closing day read the ledger's net, then open the Tags screen and note what share of this job's income each of the three tags took.
  5. Next time you quote a similar job, add those three historical shares back first, then add the margin you want. That is your floor price.

FAQ

Should I record my own hours as an expense?

No, that distorts the net. Write hours in the note instead; when the job closes, divide net by hours to get the job's real hourly rate, and use it to compare which kinds of work are worth taking.

Deposit and final payment as separate entries?

Yes. One entry each, with dates, so halfway through you know what has arrived and what is still outstanding, and the quote never gets mistaken for money already earned.

Is MoneyTag a subscription?
