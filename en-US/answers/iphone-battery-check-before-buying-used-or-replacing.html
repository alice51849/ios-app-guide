iPhone battery check: three numbers that tell you if the battery is still fine

Most people checking their battery really want to know one thing: replace it or not, or whether a used iPhone is a good buy. One percentage is not enough. Capacity, cycles and everyday drain and heat belong together.

Open Settings > Battery > Battery Health & Charging and read Maximum Capacity. Above 80% is usually still fine; below 80%, or a Service message, is the point to consider a replacement. On iPhone 15 and later the cycle count is in Settings > General > About. Then watch a week of hourly drain and heat: if capacity is high but the phone still dies fast, the cause is usually usage or an app, not battery wear.

Maximum Capacity compares what the battery can hold now with when it was new. Apple says a typical iPhone battery keeps about 80% of its original capacity after around 500 full cycles under normal conditions, and iPhone 15 models raise that to about 1,000. So a two-year-old phone at 85% is normal. What matters is dropping below 80%, performance management being applied, or a service recommendation.

A single percentage hides two things. One is the trend: two phones at 86% are very different if one lost 1% in six months and the other lost 2% in a month, and you only see that by recording over time. The other is heat: long periods of high temperature age a battery faster than charge count, such as gaming while charging or leaving the phone in a hot car. The system only reports the thermal state of the whole phone, not the battery temperature, so the useful question is when it tends to run hot.

When buying or selling a used iPhone, the battery is the most asked and least clearly answered item. A screenshot of the capacity screen does not prove which phone it came from or when. Checking Settings together in person, plus a summary that states each value's source and date, is fairer to both sides.

What to check before you choose

  • Maximum Capacity: 80% is the line most people use to decide on a replacement, not the only signal
  • Cycle count: shown in Settings on iPhone 15 and later; older models need the system analytics data
  • Trend over a single number: the same percentage means different things if it falls fast or slowly
  • Heat periods: running hot while charging or at certain times is worth fixing before anything else
  • Clear sources: know which values come from the system and which were typed in or estimated

The steps that actually work

  1. Open Settings > Battery > Battery Health & Charging and note Maximum Capacity and any service message
  2. On iPhone 15 and later note the cycle count in Settings > General > About; older models can get it from an analytics file
  3. Watch hourly drain and heat for a week to find when the battery falls fastest
  4. Adjust charging habits: avoid heavy use while charging and cap charging at 80–90% when it suits you
  5. Check Maximum Capacity again after 30 days or more and judge by how much it changed

FAQ

Should I replace a battery at 85% capacity?

Usually not. Above 80% most phones still last a normal day. More important than the number is how fast it falls, whether the phone often runs hot, and whether Settings shows a service message.

Can an app read my iPhone's Maximum Capacity directly?

No. iOS does not expose that value to third-party apps. Battery apps can record charge level, charging state and the phone's thermal state; capacity and cycle count come from your input or an imported analytics file.

Is charging to 100% bad for the battery?

Sitting at 100% for long periods, especially when warm, speeds up aging. If your routine allows, capping charging at 80–90% and not gaming while charging are the easiest habits to change.
