iPhone Maximum Capacity: read the trend, not one number

Seeing 'Maximum Capacity 87%' does not tell you whether it is falling fast or slowly. A few weeks of records show whether to change charging habits or start thinking about a replacement.

Maximum Capacity shows how much charge the battery can hold compared with when it was new, and you can check it under Battery in iPhone Settings. Settings only shows the current value, so the practical approach is to note it with the date and look at how much it drops per month. Around 80% is often cited as the point to consider a replacement, but how fast it falls and how long the phone really lasts matter more than one number.

One reading cannot tell you whether Maximum Capacity is falling quickly. Checking and recording it every two to four weeks and looking at the trend is more reliable. With a month or more between records you can compare the drop per month.

When you look back at the pace, it helps to record heat and charging habits too: whether the phone often gets hot while charging, sits at 100% for long, or charges overnight. With those habits on record, it is easier to find a reason when the number drops.

In BattAI, entering the Maximum Capacity shown in Settings gives a 0 to 100 battery health score right away, and the recorded capacity is charted with a 95% confidence band. With 30 days or more between records, the monthly capacity change is shown too. BattAI cannot read Maximum Capacity by itself, so you enter it or import it from a system analytics file (log-aggregated).

What to check before you choose

  • Today's Maximum Capacity and the date you checked it
  • How many points it dropped since the last record
  • Whether 30 days or more separate your records
  • Whether the phone often gets hot while charging
  • Whether it often sits at 100% for long

The steps that actually work

  1. Check today's Maximum Capacity under Battery in Settings and note it with the date.
  2. Use the phone as usual while recording charging and heat habits.
  3. Two to four weeks later, check Maximum Capacity again and note it.
  4. Once records span 30 days or more, check the drop per month.
  5. Look at the pace and real battery life to decide between changing habits and considering a replacement.
