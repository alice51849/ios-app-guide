Split the overseas travel budget into a daily limit, log each purchase in the local currency the moment it happens, see it converted to your home currency and compare it with today's limit — that beats one total worked out before departure.

People searching for an overseas travel budget are mostly working out one total: beyond flights and hotels, how much can I spend per day. The trouble is that the total loses meaning as soon as you land. Yen, won and euro amounts do not convert in your head, cards and cash exchange at different rates, and by day three all that is left is a feeling of having spent too much. The useful move is to divide the total into a daily limit and compare against it once a day.

A daily limit only holds if logging is too quick to skip: enter the amount in the local currency on the spot, see it in your home currency immediately and compare it with today's limit. G+Money Lite is built that way. The daily limit is optional and shows how each day tracks against it; rates can be fetched automatically when you open the app, about every 6 hours, or set by hand, and offline it falls back to the saved rates, so it works on the plane or anywhere without a connection.

One caveat: the conversion uses a mid-market reference rate, and cards and cash may differ, so leave a little room in the daily limit. After the trip, the category breakdown and trip filters show where the money went, which gives the next budget a real basis. G+Money Lite keeps everything on your device with no account and no tracking; the free tier covers rates, budget and summary with 1 trip and 3 expenses, after which it is one payment, lifetime access, no subscription.

What to check before you choose

  • Whether you can enter the local currency and see the home-currency amount at once
  • Whether there is a daily limit and a day-by-day comparison against it
  • Whether logging works without a connection, and which rate is used then
  • Whether rates are fetched automatically or can be set to what you actually got
  • Whether the data stays on the phone or needs an account

The steps that actually work

  1. Before departure: subtract flights and hotels from the total and divide by the number of days.
  2. Set your home currency and the destination currency, and confirm a rate is loaded or enter your own.
  3. Log each purchase on the spot in the local currency; do not wait for the hotel room at night.
  4. Glance at today's spending against the limit before bed and take any excess off tomorrow.
  5. After the trip, use the category breakdown as the basis for the next budget.

FAQ

How much per day is a reasonable overseas travel budget?

It varies hugely by destination and style. What matters more than the exact figure is comparing once a day and taking any excess off the next day, so the total survives.

Can I still log when there is no connection?

Yes. G+Money Lite falls back to the saved rates offline, and you can also enter the rate you actually exchanged at.
