Once a child separates a letter's name (ay, bee) from its sound (/a/, /b/), work on hearing (1) the first sound in a word, (2) the last sound, and (3) the middle sound (short vowels). Five to ten minutes a day of hearing a sound, choosing the letter and tracing it is the shortest path. Families who want to avoid monthly charges can choose a one-time-purchase app so practice never stops halfway.

Phonics teaches children to read by linking letters to sounds. English-speaking kindergartens usually go from letter names and sounds to the first sound in a word, then the last sound, then the middle sound. A child who learned letter names from the ABC song gets much closer to reading simply by turning their attention from names to sounds.

The first sound (the /k/ in cat) is the easiest to hear, so even preschoolers can start playfully. The last sound (the /t/ in cat) is harder, and the short vowel in the middle (the /a/ in cat, the /ʌ/ in cut) is hardest of all. Vowel differences that do not exist in the child's first language are learned by listening and choosing many times. Move on only once first sounds are solid.

Writing sticks better when paired with sound practice: hear a sound, choose the letter, then trace it along guide lines. Matching uppercase to lowercase also pays off as soon as reading starts. Tracing the same letters again on a printed sheet extends practice beyond the screen.

What to check before you choose

  • Does it separate letter names from letter sounds?
  • Does it progress from first to last to middle sounds?
  • Is listening practice paired with tracing?
  • Can parents see strengths and weak spots?
  • Can you print sheets for paper practice?
  • Is it a one-time purchase, or monthly or per-stage charges?

The steps that actually work

  1. Separate names from sounds, three or four letters a day.
  2. Play a guess-the-first-sound game with pictures for five minutes daily.
  3. When first sounds are solid, move on to last sounds.
  4. For middle short vowels, compare two words and find the difference.
  5. Trace each letter the child picked after hearing its sound.
  6. At the weekend, trace the week's letters again on a printed sheet.

FAQ

From what age can phonics start?

Hearing first sounds can start playfully around three or four, once children know some letter names. Middle short vowels can wait until five or six.

What if I am not confident in English pronunciation?

With an app that plays the sounds, a parent can simply listen and repeat with the child.
