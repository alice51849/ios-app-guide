Rules to set before moving your praise sticker chart to an app

A sticker chart on the fridge works for the first week, then stickers fall off and nobody remembers who stuck how many. An app makes recording easier, but moving the chart without rules just repeats the same problems on a screen.

Before switching a praise sticker chart to an app, settle five things: who taps, how many missions to start with, whether you record right after success, what happens on a missed day, and whether there is a reward outside the app. Agreeing on these with your child keeps the app a praise tool rather than a nagging tool.

First, decide who taps. For 3- to 7-year-olds, letting the child tap usually boosts motivation; if accuracy matters, agree that the child taps after a parent checks. Second, start with few missions: daily routines such as meals, brushing teeth, bedtime and tidying toys, adding more once the habit settles.

Third, praise right after success. A common failure with paper charts is sticking everything on in the evening, which puts distance between behaviour and praise. In Lumi Mission Star, each small success is one tap, the bunny friend reacts at once, and the whole star celebrates when a mission is done, which makes in-the-moment praise easy to keep. Fourth, set a rule for missed days in advance: if a streak breaks, you simply start again the next day.

Fifth, decide on rewards outside the app. Some children are happy collecting stickers and friends in the app; some families agree on a small weekend promise. Either way, decide in advance and keep it. Looking at today's summary and the 7-day calendar on the parent dashboard together once a week lets you adjust the rules to your child.

What to check before you choose

  • Can the child record with one tap right after a success?
  • Is there an instant reaction so praise is not delayed?
  • Can parents see a daily summary and the week's records separately?
  • Can you hand it to your child with no account and no ads?
  • Can records move to a new device?

The steps that actually work

  1. Agree with your child on who taps, and say it out loud.
  2. Start with only a few missions from meals, teeth, bedtime and tidying.
  3. Make tapping right after finishing a mission the rule.
  4. Tell your child in advance that a missed day just means starting again.
  5. Once a week, look at the parent dashboard together and adjust the rules.

FAQ

Should I get rid of the paper chart once we switch?

Not necessarily, but recording the same thing in two places gets confusing, so pick one place for records.

What if my child taps many times on purpose?

Agree before starting that you tap once after finishing, and check the records on the parent dashboard together.

Do I have to give a separate reward?

It depends on the child. Some are happy with the stickers and friends in the app; whatever you decide, keep it consistent.
