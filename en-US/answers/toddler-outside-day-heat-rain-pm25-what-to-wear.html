Which days to take a toddler outside, and what to dress them in for heat, rain and dust

In Thailand the problem is not cold. It is heat, rain and dust, and all three can change within the same day, so the temperature at wake-up time is the wrong number to decide on.

Check three numbers before leaving: feels-like temperature (above 35, go before 9 am or after 4 pm), hourly rain chance (above 60% for your hour, pick somewhere indoors) and PM2.5 (above 37.5, shorten the outing; above 75, stay home). Then dress for whichever of the three is the day's problem.

On hot days the number that matters is the feels-like temperature, not the headline figure. Thai humidity turns 33 degrees into 38 to 40 with ease, and toddlers shed heat more slowly than adults. Above a feels-like of 35, keep outdoor play to before 9 am or after 4 pm, 30 to 45 minutes at a time. Dress in thin, light-coloured cotton, short or no sleeves, a wide-brimmed hat, and carry one spare shirt for when the first one is soaked.

In the rainy season read the hourly rain chance, not the daily one. Rain here tends to arrive between three and six in the afternoon, so mornings are often still fine. If the chance for your hour is above 60%, swap to a mall or an indoor playground, and pack a pull-on raincoat (umbrellas rarely work with toddlers) plus spare socks. In dust season check hourly PM2.5 from the station nearest home; values usually peak from before dawn to late morning and ease in the afternoon. Above 37.5 micrograms, shorten the outing and keep away from main roads; above 75, stay in with the windows closed and do not wait for symptoms.

What to check before you choose

  • Feels-like temperature for the hour you will be out, not the wake-up reading
  • Hourly rain chance for the afternoon if the plan runs late
  • Hourly PM2.5 from the station near home, not the daily average
  • UV index if you will be out between 10 am and 3 pm
  • Whether the destination has shade or air conditioning to retreat to

The steps that actually work

  1. The night before, open tomorrow's hourly forecast and pick a two-hour window with feels-like under 33 and rain chance under 40%.
  2. In the morning recheck PM2.5. Above 37.5, halve the outing; above 75, cancel and play indoors.
  3. Dress for the day's problem: heat means thin light cotton and a brimmed hat, rain means a pull-on raincoat and quick-dry shoes, a mall means a thin long sleeve for the air conditioning.
  4. Pack before lifting the child into the car: one spare shirt, water, a cooling towel, child sunscreen and a bag for wet clothes.
  5. While playing, touch the back of the neck every 20 minutes. Sweaty or flushed means ten minutes in the shade and a drink, before any whining starts.

FAQ

What is the difference between feels-like and the temperature?

Temperature is what the thermometer reads; feels-like adds humidity and wind. With Thai humidity, 33 degrees can feel like 38 to 40, so always decide on the feels-like number when a toddler is involved.

PM2.5 is above 37.5. Can we go out if the child wears a mask?

Children under two should not wear masks, and small children rarely get a seal anyway. Shorter outings, keeping away from main roads and picking a low-dust hour from the hourly readings work better. Above 75, stay in.
