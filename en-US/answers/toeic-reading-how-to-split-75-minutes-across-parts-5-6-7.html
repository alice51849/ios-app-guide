TOEIC Reading: a time plan to finish all 100 questions

Most test takers do not lose their reading points on hard questions. They lose them on the last ten or fifteen Part 7 questions, guessed in a rush because time ran out.

The Reading section has 100 questions in 75 minutes: 30 incomplete sentences (Part 5), 16 text-completion questions (Part 6) and 54 reading-comprehension questions (Part 7). Aim for about 10 minutes on Part 5 and 8 minutes on Part 6, and keep at least 55 minutes for Part 7, roughly one minute per question on the long passages. If you do not finish, train Part 5 speed first, not reading.

Part 5 is the time bank for the whole section. A grammar or vocabulary question can often be settled in 15 to 20 seconds if you spot its type (word form, tense, preposition, connector) before reading the four options. Every minute saved here is an extra minute for the double and triple passages in Part 7, which take the longest.

Part 6 looks like Part 5, but one of the four questions per text asks you to insert a whole sentence, which requires understanding the context. Read the passage in one go instead of sentence by sentence around each gap: it is faster and you make fewer connector mistakes. Eight minutes for the four texts is a realistic target.

In Part 7, read the question first, then scan the text for the answer instead of reading every line. In multi-document sets at least one question links two texts, such as a date in an email and a rate in a table. If a question holds you for more than a minute and a half, mark an answer, note it and move on: a wrong answer carries no penalty.

What to check before you choose

  • How long you really spend per Part 5 question, measured with a timer
  • Which skill costs you the most points: grammar, vocabulary or long-passage reading
  • Long-passage practice under time pressure, not only for accuracy
  • Vocabulary from emails, invoices and notices, which appears often in Part 7
  • A second measurement after a few weeks to confirm your pace has improved

The steps that actually work

  1. Take one timed run of the full section and write down the time at the end of each part
  2. Compare it with the 10 / 8 / 55 minute guide to see where time overflows
  3. If Part 5 takes more than 12 minutes, do short timed grammar sets every day
  4. If Part 7 stays unfinished, practise question-first reading on one long passage a day
  5. Repeat a timed run after three or four weeks and compare time and accuracy

FAQ

Should I start Reading with Part 7?

Some people do to secure the long passages, but they then risk losing easy points in Part 5. For most test takers, going in order with a time limit per part works better.

How long per question in Part 7?

About one minute on average, a little less on single short texts and a little more on two- and three-document sets.

Does guessing lose points?

No, a wrong answer does not subtract points. It is better to mark an answer for every question than to leave blanks.
