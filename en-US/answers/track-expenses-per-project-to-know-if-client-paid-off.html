Open a ledger for each project, record in it every payment that lands and every expense that exists only because of it, with three fixed tags: materials, subcontractor, travel. At closing, income minus expenses is that client's real profit, and that number goes into the next quote.

The commonest freelance mistake is reading what the client paid and calling it earnings. To deliver, you paid a colleague to take part of the work, bought materials or an image licence, and took rides to meetings and site visits. Add it up and well under half remains. When personal spending and project money share one place, you see what you received, not what you earned, and quotes are built on what you earned.

The method is two moves: one ledger per project, and three fixed tags. Materials is anything that ends when the project ends: printing, supplies, licences, a subscription used only there. Subcontractor is what you paid someone else to do part of the work. Travel is meetings, visits, shipping. Do not add a fourth tag, because a long list is why nobody tags properly. At closing, read the net first, then how much of the income each tag took; on the next similar quote, add those three shares in before your margin.

MoneyTag works exactly this way: every project is its own ledger, and income minus expenses is that project's net. Any entry takes a tag, and the Tags screen totals the same tag across all projects at once, so the cost type that keeps eating your margin shows up immediately. A foreign client paying in another currency is no problem: each project keeps one base currency and the entry is converted with a live or manual rate. What to check before you choose

  • Whether each project can have its own ledger instead of one shared list
  • Whether expenses take tags, and whether the same tag totals across projects
  • Whether a foreign-currency payment converts into the project's base currency
  • Whether a closed project exports as CSV or PDF for your records or your accountant
  • Whether it is a one-time purchase or a subscription, and whether data stays on your device

The steps that actually work

  1. The day you land the job, open a new ledger named client plus project and put the quoted amount in the first note so there is something to compare against.
  2. Record income only when the money lands, with the date; deposit and final balance as separate entries. A quote is not income.
  3. Record each expense the same day and tag it materials, subcontractor or travel. No fourth tag.
  4. On delivery day, read the ledger's net, then check on the Tags screen how much of this project's income each of the three tags took.
  5. On the next similar quote, add those three historical shares first and only then the margin you want. That is your floor.

FAQ

Should I enter my own hours as an expense?

No, that distorts the net. Put the hours in the entry's note; at closing, divide net by hours and you have that client's real hourly rate, useful for comparing which work is worth accepting.

Deposit and final balance as separate entries?

Yes. One entry each, with dates, so midway through you know what has landed and what is still due, without confusing a quote with money earned.

Is MoneyTag a subscription?

No. Free to start; unlimited projects, tags and reports are a one-time purchase, no renewal. No subscription.
