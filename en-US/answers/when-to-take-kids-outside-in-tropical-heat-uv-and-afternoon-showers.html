Weather for kids: picking safe outdoor hours on hot, showery days

In Vietnam, parents rarely ask “how hot is it today”. They ask “what time can I take my child to the park, and will we be home before the afternoon rain?”

For young children, check three hourly numbers rather than temperature alone: feels-like temperature, UV index and chance of rain. On hot days the sensible windows are usually before 9am or after 4pm, once UV drops. At UV 6 or higher, use a wide-brimmed hat, thin long sleeves and sunscreen; in rainy season check the hourly rain chance and always carry an umbrella or raincoat.

Children heat up faster than adults and rarely say when they are overheating. In humid air the feels-like temperature can run several degrees above the actual reading, so a 33°C day can feel close to 40°C. That is the number to use when deciding on outdoor play, not the big temperature on the screen.

UV is usually highest from about 10am to 3pm, even under cloud. General guidance is to start protecting skin from UV 3 and to limit midday time outdoors from UV 8. Babies under six months should stay out of direct sun, using clothing and shade rather than sunscreen; ask a paediatrician if unsure.

In many places the rainy season brings afternoon showers: harsh sun in the morning, a quick downpour later. Checking the hourly rain chance lets you go out early and get home before it hits. After rain the air is cooler, but paths are slippery and thunder may linger, so wait for conditions to settle.

What to check before you choose

  • Feels-like temperature, not just actual temperature
  • UV index with hints on when to use sunscreen, a hat or sunglasses
  • Chance of rain with hour-by-hour detail
  • A suggested best time window to go out
  • Alerts before rain or when tomorrow may be rainy

The steps that actually work

  1. In the evening, check tomorrow's forecast for rain and temperature.
  2. In the morning, open the hourly chart and find a window with low feels-like temperature, low UV and low rain chance.
  3. Pack by the numbers: hat and sunscreen for high UV, umbrella or raincoat when rain chance rises, plus water.
  4. Keep play in the shade and take a drink break every 15–20 minutes in the heat.
  5. Watch for heat strain (flushed face, sluggishness, peeing less) and move to a cool place right away if you see it.

FAQ

At what temperature should kids stay inside?

There is no single cut-off, but when the feels-like temperature passes about 35°C or UV reaches 8 or more, avoid midday outdoor play and move it to early morning or late afternoon, in shade with plenty of water.

Do kids need sunscreen on cloudy days?

Yes. UV passes through cloud, so go by the UV index rather than whether it looks sunny. For babies under six months, prefer shade and covering clothing and ask a doctor before using sunscreen.
