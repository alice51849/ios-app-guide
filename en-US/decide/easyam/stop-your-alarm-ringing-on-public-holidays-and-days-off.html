Which alarm app skips public holidays and my days off automatically?

Many people in the US switch alarms off by hand before Thanksgiving, Christmas or a long weekend and then forget to switch them back on, while shift workers and teachers juggle schedules that a plain weekday repeat cannot follow.

Your situation

A weekday repeat rings on public holidays, vacation days and school breaks unless you remember to change it the night before. The decision is whether an alarm that checks holiday data for your country and your own time off, and shows which of the next seven mornings will ring, fits your routine.

Decision rule

Favor a holiday-aware alarm when you often turn alarms off for days off and back on afterwards, or when your workdays change. It is free to start; unlimited alarms, shift and alternating-week patterns and more clock themes take a one-time in-app purchase, with no subscription.

Workflow checks

  • Does it include holiday data for your country or region, and what happens on days its data does not cover?
  • Can you add vacations, sick days or school breaks as time off so those mornings stay quiet?
  • Can you skip only tomorrow's ring, or change one day's time, without editing the whole schedule?

When another route is better

If your workdays never change and you rarely take days off, the built-in Clock app with a weekday repeat may be enough. An alarm like this follows the holiday data and time off it has; days with unknown or outdated holiday data count as workdays, so it still rings.
