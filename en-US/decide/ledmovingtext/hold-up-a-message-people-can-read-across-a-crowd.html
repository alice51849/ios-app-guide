How do I turn an iPhone screen into a sign people can read from across a crowd?

US arrivals halls, standing-room venues and busy service counters all need a name or an instruction that reads from far away, and the phone already in hand is usually the only screen there.

Your situation

A note typed into a notes app stops being readable past the first row, and a printed card cannot be changed once the name, gate or order number changes. The decision is whether a phone screen can carry one message at a readable size, and whether the next message can be queued instead of retyped between moments.

Decision rule

Choose a full-screen scrolling board when the message has to be read at a distance and is likely to change during the same event. The board itself is free to try, and the extra controls for running several prepared messages are one one-time unlock, not a subscription.

Workflow checks

  • Can the message still be read from the back of the room or the far end of an arrivals hall?
  • Can several prepared messages be stepped through without leaving the full-screen board?
  • Can a message be corrected on the spot when a name, gate or order number changes?

When another route is better

A printed sign is still better in outdoor daylight, or when the phone has to stay free for calls. A dedicated LED panel is better when one unchanging message has to run unattended for hours.
