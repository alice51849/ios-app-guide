What time do I need to enter a Taipei MRT station to arrive on time?

Many travelers from the US riding the Taipei MRT for the first time, and people living in Taiwan on a daily commute, know when they need to arrive for a meeting, a dinner or the last train of the night, but not when they need to walk into the station, especially when the trip includes a transfer.

Your situation

Most transit apps answer when you will arrive if you leave now. Before a meeting or the last train of the night, the question is the reverse: the latest time to enter the station, with every transfer counted. The decision is whether a planner that works backward from your arrival time, using official published headways and travel times between stations, fits how you ride.

Decision rule

Favor a leave-by planner when you often ride toward a fixed arrival time, use the Xiaobitan or Xinbeitou branch, or want the planner in a language you know. You can use every feature for 24 hours; after that, a single one-time unlock keeps the full app, with no subscription and nothing that renews automatically.

Workflow checks

  • Does it show the latest time to enter the station, with a step-by-step timeline for every leg and transfer?
  • Does it warn you about first and last trains and remind you when it is time to go?
  • Does it cover the branch you ride, such as Xiaobitan or Xinbeitou, with the official fixed timetable?

When another route is better

If you need live train arrivals, or you plan to ride the Airport MRT or light rail, a general map or transit app may fit better. The times in a leave-by planner like this are estimates based on official published headways and travel times between stations, not live train arrivals.
