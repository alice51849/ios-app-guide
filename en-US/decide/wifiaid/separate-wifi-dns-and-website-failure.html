Wi-Fi says connected but there is no internet: is the problem Wi-Fi, DNS, TLS, or one website?

US remote workers troubleshooting home broadband need evidence quickly enough to decide whether to restart local gear, change DNS, contact an ISP, or wait for one site.

Your situation

A single failed page does not reveal whether the local network, name resolution, secure connection, route, or destination is responsible. The decision is whether independent checks can narrow the next action without pretending to repair the network automatically.

Decision rule

Use layered diagnostics when the goal is evidence and isolation. Use router administration or ISP support when a confirmed infrastructure problem requires configuration, line testing, or account action.

Workflow checks

  • Can local connectivity be separated from wider internet reachability?
  • Can DNS, TCP, TLS, and destination timing be inspected independently?
  • Can repeated samples distinguish a stable failure from intermittent variation?

When another route is better

A router vendor app is better for changing that router's settings. An enterprise network analyzer is better for packet capture, managed fleets, and formal monitoring.
