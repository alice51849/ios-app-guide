An alarm that skips public holidays: what to check

A fixed weekday alarm rings on the public holiday you wanted to sleep through and, worse, can stay silent on the make-up workday you must not miss. The usual workaround is to switch the alarm off by hand before every holiday and remember to switch it back on. A better alarm works out your real workdays from your country and region: it skips public holidays, rings on official make-up workdays, and respects the time off you enter yourself.

What to look for

  • Public holidays skipped and make-up workdays rung, for your own country and region.
  • Real system alarms through AlarmKit rather than a notification that can be missed.
  • An unknown holiday date treated as a workday, never as a day off.
  • Weekly, alternating-week or shift patterns, plus your own time off.
  • Holiday data that works offline and shows how far ahead your alarms are set.

FAQ

Will it ring on a make-up workday?

Yes. Official make-up workdays are added to your schedule and ring even when they fall on a weekend; a date the app cannot confirm is treated as a workday.

Does it work offline?

Yes. The holiday data is built into the app, so alarms are scheduled without a connection; newer data can be downloaded when you are online.

Is it a real alarm?

It schedules the upcoming rings as individual alarms with Apple's AlarmKit, so iOS rings them, and it reads the schedule back from iOS before showing it as ready.

Is there a subscription?

No. The download includes one saved alarm, and a single one-time in-app purchase unlocks unlimited alarms, shift and alternating-week patterns and more clock themes.
