How to Diagnose Slow WiFi on iPhone (Beyond 'Turn It Off and On Again')

When WiFi is slow, the cause could be your router, your ISP, a specific website or app's server, or interference from nearby networks. Without diagnostics, you're guessing. WiFi Aid runs independent checks to narrow down exactly where the problem is.

Step by step

  1. Open WiFi Aid when you notice the connection is slow

    You don't need to be a networking expert. WiFi Aid is designed for people who want clear answers, not raw data.

  2. Run the connection path check

    WiFi Aid tests DNS resolution, TCP handshake and TLS response independently. This tells you whether the problem is local (your router or ISP) or remote (the server you're trying to reach).

  3. Read the plain-language result

    The app tells you which part of the path is slow or failing — not just 'there is a problem', but where.

  4. Take action based on the finding

    If DNS is slow, try changing your DNS server. If TCP fails, the issue is likely your ISP or router. If TLS fails on a specific site, that site's server may be having issues.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need technical knowledge to use WiFi Aid?

No. WiFi Aid is designed for everyday users. The results are shown in plain language with clear explanations.

Does WiFi Aid upload my network data?

WiFi Aid runs diagnostics on-device and does not upload your network topology or results to any server.

Does it fix the problem automatically?

WiFi Aid diagnoses and explains. Fixing the problem may require adjusting your router settings, contacting your ISP, or waiting for a remote server to recover — things no iPhone app can do for you.
