คำตอบสั้น

จู้ยิน (Zhuyin) was created in the 1910s, decades before Pinyin (1950s), and became embedded in Taiwan's schooling: children learn it first, and kids' books print it alongside characters as a reading bridge. Beyond history, its 37 non-Latin symbols keep the pronunciation aid visually separate from both English and the Chinese characters, which many teachers find gives cleaner, more accurate pronunciation. It is not that one system is right — Pinyin is the global standard for typing and international learners — but for a Taiwanese-style start, จู้ยิน (Zhuyin) is the path. FAQ

Is จู้ยิน (Zhuyin) only used in Taiwan?

Largely yes — Taiwan uses จู้ยิน (Zhuyin) as its main phonetic system, while Mainland China and most international courses use Pinyin. If your family uses Taiwanese books or wants that pronunciation style, จู้ยิน (Zhuyin) is the route Lumi Bopomofo supports.

Should international learners use จู้ยิน (Zhuyin)?

It depends on your goals. For typing and global resources, Pinyin is more practical. For accurate pronunciation with minimal English interference, or a Taiwanese context, จู้ยิน (Zhuyin) helps. Many learners meet Pinyin first and add จู้ยิน (Zhuyin) later.
