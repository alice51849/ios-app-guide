How it works

  1. Dates are compared at local midnight in UTC terms, so daylight-saving changes never add or lose a day.
  2. “Count the end date” adds one day, the way a hotel stay or a course that includes both dates is counted.
  3. Months are counted one calendar month at a time; when a month is shorter the step lands on its last day, so 31 January to 1 March is 1 month and 1 day in a non-leap year.
  4. The Monday–Friday figure simply skips Saturdays and Sundays; it does not know your public holidays.

What it will never do

  • It does not know public holidays or school calendars, so treat the Monday–Friday count as a ceiling.
  • It does not send reminders or save your events — close the tab and they are gone.
  • No account, no tracking, nothing uploaded.

Questions

Does it count the start date?

The plain count is the number of midnights between the two dates. Choose “Count the end date: yes” when both the first and the last day should be counted, for example for a 1–7 March holiday.

Why is the Monday–Friday number different from my working days?

Because it does not remove public holidays or your own days off. Subtract those yourself.

Can I count backwards?

Yes. If the end date is earlier than the start date the result is shown with a minus sign, and the add-days box accepts negative numbers.
