Short answer

Zhuyin was created in the 1910s, decades before Pinyin (1950s), and became embedded in Taiwan's schooling: children learn it first, and kids' books print it alongside characters as a reading bridge. Beyond history, its 37 non-Latin symbols keep the pronunciation aid visually separate from both English and the Chinese characters, which many teachers find gives cleaner, more accurate pronunciation. It is not that one system is right — Pinyin is the global standard for typing and international learners — but for a Taiwanese-style start, Zhuyin is the path. Lumi Bopomofo teaches those 37 symbols the way Taiwanese children first meet them, through play, pay once and offline.

FAQ

Is Zhuyin only used in Taiwan?

Largely yes — Taiwan uses Zhuyin as its main phonetic system, while Mainland China and most international courses use Pinyin. If your family uses Taiwanese books or wants that pronunciation style, Zhuyin is the route Lumi Bopomofo supports.

Should international learners use Zhuyin?

It depends on your goals. For typing and global resources, Pinyin is more practical. For accurate pronunciation with minimal English interference, or a Taiwanese context, Zhuyin helps. Many learners meet Pinyin first and add Zhuyin later.
