簡短回答

The honest answer depends on the type and severity of blur, and the limits are grounded in physics: blur is an averaging process that destroys image information, and software cannot reconstruct data that was never recorded by the sensor in the first place. AI tools can make blurry images look perceptibly sharper by generating plausible-looking replacements based on patterns from millions of training images — but those added details are the AI's best guess, not a true recovery; on a severely blurred subject they may be simply wrong. Mild motion blur (camera shake during a clear shot) responds best; heavy defocus, extreme motion blur, or low-resolution source images have hard limits no current tool overcomes.

挑選前要注意什麼

  • Motion blur (camera shake): more mathematically reversible in theory if the blur direction is known; AI tools help with mild-to-moderate cases
  • Out-of-focus/defocus blur: high-frequency detail was never captured by the sensor — AI fills in plausible edges and textures that may not match reality
  • Low-res/pixelation: AI generates convincing-looking texture but is inventing detail, not recovering it — the hallucinated pixels may be inaccurate
  • Severe blur of any type: no current software reliably recovers it — this is an information-theory limit, not a tech gap that will be closed soon
  • Faces and text tend to benefit most from AI enhancement (most training data); unusual objects, fine detail, and text-heavy backgrounds are most prone to AI errors

常見問題

Is unblurring the same as sharpening?

No. Sharpening increases contrast at edge boundaries, making an image look crisper — but it creates no new information and can introduce halos. Deblurring algorithms attempt mathematical reversal of the blur process. AI enhancement generates new pixels based on learned patterns. These are three different operations with different results and limitations.

Can you really 'enhance' a blurry photo to read a license plate, like on TV?

No — TV depictions of image enhancement are fiction. In a severely blurred or low-resolution image, the detail for a license plate was never captured. Any 'enhancement' that produces readable text from truly blurred source data is AI invention, not extraction, and would be considered fabricated evidence in a forensic or legal context.

What is the best-case scenario for unblurring a photo?

A mildly motion-blurred photo with good original lighting and a clear subject — for example, a slightly shaky handheld shot at a party. In this case, AI tools can produce a noticeably sharper result that closely reflects the actual scene. The further a photo is from this ideal, the less reliable the output.
